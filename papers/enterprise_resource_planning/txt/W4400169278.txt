Abstract
The integration of Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) and Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) in Supply Chain
Management (SCM) significantly enhances supply chain efficiency and effectiveness. ERP systems enable companies to
optimize inventory and production management, while RFID technology facilitates precise tracking of products and raw
materials, thereby improving production process efficiency. This research aims to systematically review the existing literature
on implementing ERP and RFID technologies in SCM and their interconnected roles. Utilizing the Systematic Literature
Review (SLR) methodology, the study highlights the crucial impact of ERP and RFID on supply chain performance efficiency.
The findings indicate that ERP implementation in SCM offers numerous benefits, which is vital in boosting company efficiency,
effectiveness, and overall performance. Additionally, integrating ERP and RFID with technologies like blockchain and cloud
computing can further optimize supply chain management processes, providing even greater advantages.
Keywords: Enterprise Resource Planning, Radio Frequency Identification, Supply chain Management, Systematic Literature
Review

1

Introduction

Industry 4.0 brings concepts that focus on the automation of systems, processes, digitalization, and exchange in
the industrial world, aiming to achieve smart factories that can reduce lead times in response to customer demand,
cope with unexpected events, and increase productivity in the system. Industry 4.0 enforces fundamental changes
in the manufacturing processes of today's companies. Integrating digitalization and the Internet into the
manufacturing process leads to the global transformation of the manufacturing industry (Paksoy et al., 2021).
Supply chain management (SCM) is almost all companies' most important business process. To integrate all supply
chain actors, SCM uses processes and technologies that have continued to evolve over the years (Hader et al.,
2020). There are very important technological advances to improve supply chain processes, for example, ERP and
RFID technology. Two technologies that can be utilized in supply chain management are radio frequency
identification (RFID) and enterprise resource planning (ERP).
Integrating ERP into supply chain management facilitates improved coordination between the supply chain and
different departments within an organization (Koh et al. 2014). Information technology plays a vital role in shaping
a competitive business strategy and is considered an essential component. Enterprise resource planning (ERP) is a
form of information technology that provides a framework for reorganizing business processes (Acar et al. 2017).

Enterprise resource planning systems bring together information across all parts of the organization, both internal
and external, for a smoother flow of information. It can be one fully integrated system, several partially integrated
systems, or many best-in-class systems, depending on the wants and needs of the organization (Trivedi et al. 2018).
Enterprise resource planning systems enhance and improve data sharing. Factors that hinder production can also
be identified easily through ERP systems. The availability of information provided by ERP enables better decisionmaking in terms of production processes, inventory control, and many other aspects (Koh et al. 2014). ERP systems
used effectively will provide current, vital information for decision-making. The role of accounting has also
become easier due to the timely availability of financial information in the supply chain, thus making the
accounting process easier. Supply chain management practices can be carried out more efficiently and profitably
(Sawahir and Roshan, 2021).
RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is an emerging technology that uses radiofrequency electromagnetic
fields to facilitate the contactless and wireless data transmission required for automatic identification and tracking
of tags attached to various items (Raki, 2014). This technology enables unique identification of objects and
seamless tracking throughout their lifecycle. RFID tags, which are equipped with microchips and antennas, can be
attached to various objects, such as products, assets, or even living organisms, enabling efficient monitoring and
management in various applications including supply chain management, inventory control, asset tracking, and
access control systems. By leveraging RFID technology, organizations can increase operational efficiency,
improve inventory accuracy, streamline processes, and improve overall visibility and control over their assets and
resources. RFID is built on the software and hardware platform of wireless sensor networks and the detailed design
of the business processes of logistics supply chain management systems (Pan and Liu, 2021).RFID technology is
widely used in the company's field or inventory section (supply). Value creation stems from the conversion of data
resources through enterprise technology, and RFID emerges as a capability that yields substantial advantages for
knowledge management systems, thereby enhancing the supply chain. The adoption of RFID by enterprises
represents targeted resource investments to bolster capabilities and improve the overall supply chain (Reyes and
Worthington, 2015). We can encourage people to actively control every area of the logistics supply chain by
combining wireless sensor networks and RFID technologies with the model for managing the logistics supply
chain. This strategy intends to raise the logistics supply chain management standard, lower logistics-related
expenses and security oversight, and ultimately provide customers with secure and superior logistics products.
Future developments in logistics will mostly be driven by incorporating cutting-edge information technologies,
such as RFID and wireless sensor networks, into supply chain management practices. Its relevance stems from its
ability to propel the growth of supply chain management logistics (Pan and Liu, 2021).
Integrating an ERP system into a business represents a potent strategy for enhancing operational efficiency and
augmenting organizational efficacy (Surung et al., 2020). As Linda et al. (2022) indicated, ERP exerts a direct and
favorable influence on supply chain management (SCM), positively impacting organizational performance.
Combining ERP with SCM practices can elevate a company's competitiveness and overall performance (Ince et
al., 2013). Numerous studies focusing on Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID) in SCM underscore its substantial
contributions to a company's knowledge management system, a pivotal pathway to enhancing supply chain
performance (Reyes and Worthington, 2015). The synergy of ERP and RFID within SCM holds promise for
enhancing supply chain efficiency and effectiveness. ERP enables companies to optimize inventory and production
management, while RFID facilitates product and raw material tracking, thereby enhancing production process
efficiency and effectiveness. Leveraging electronic solutions such as RFID and ERP can enhance supply chain
integration, a fundamental SCM practice. Collaboration among supply chain stakeholders ensures the smooth and
productive flow of goods, capital, and information across the entire network of suppliers, manufacturers,
distributors, and customers. The overarching objective is to maximize benefits for the entire supply chain
ecosystem (Oghazi et al., 2018).
This study focuses on ERP and RFID, which can contribute to the supply chain process. This article aims to
systematically review the literature on implementing ERP and RFID technologies in supply chain management
and the integration between them. To achieve this goal, three sub-research questions (SRQs) are formulated and
discussed specifically, namely: (1) ERP system implementation in SCM; (2) RFID system implementation in SCM;
and (3) integration between ERP and RFID systems in SCM. In addition, this study will also present the
development or evolution related to research trends on the topic of ERP and RFID system implementation in SCM
using bibliometric analysis.
Additionally, this research aims to thoroughly examine the utilization of ERP and RFID systems within the
supply chain management domain. The study is anticipated to provide a comprehensive and in-depth review of
how these systems are employed, their effectiveness, and their impact on various aspects of supply chain
management. This study will uncover trends, influencing factors, benefits, and drawbacks in implementing these
technologies by analyzing many relevant scientific articles. Using the SLR (Systematic Literature Review) method
in this study will update the existing knowledge on this topic. By covering the latest articles, this study will provide

Literature Review

2.1 Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)
ERP stands for Enterprise Resource Planning, a useful tool for managing various business processes in an
integrated manner in one unit, including marketing, production, purchasing, and accounting. ERP can also store
all transactions made by the company in a database that the company can access and provides tools for management
information reporting (Chandra et al., 2022). According to Maulana and Paryogo (2020), the ERP system is a
system that supports business processes by providing real-time information that is integrated between functional
divisions of the company. An enterprise resource planning (ERP) system is a valuable tool for integrating
information from various organizational departments and consolidating data into a unified database.
The database can be stored locally if the ERP system is implemented within the company's physical location or
cloud-based if the ERP system is implemented externally, outside the company's infrastructure (Surung et al.,
2020). Roshan (2021) asserts that enterprise systems, commonly called ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)
systems, are software solutions that integrate various functions, including financial management, accounting,
human resources, customer relations, supply chain, manufacturing, marketing, sales, and distribution. Their main
purpose is synchronizing information exchange and facilitating efficient organizational coordination and
collaboration. These ERP systems support repetitive and continuous business processes, such as supply chain
management, order management, and general payment transactions..
According to Amadi-Echendu (2023), ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) is a module or application that can
fundamentally change an organization's business operations. It is a software system specifically designed to
integrate various business processes within an organization. ERP modules or applications cover many functions,
including financial management, human resource management, inventory management, production management,
logistics management, and more.
When various companies face a number of organizationally independent information systems, they usually face
the accumulation of large amounts of non-standardized information across different departments, functions, and
business processes throughout the company that cannot be transferred due to their heterogeneous formats (Oghazi,
2009). For this reason, various companies use ERP systems to link information from different segments of the
organization with each other to facilitate proper and timely delivery, improve customer satisfaction, and reduce
costs (Tsai et al., 2007). Using ERP, an organization can combine various aspects of its business into one integrated
platform, enabling seamless data exchange between different departments and improving operational coordination
and efficiency. In addition, the implementation of ERP in companies has many benefits. By implementing ERP,
companies are able to increase their productivity by reducing expenses and increasing revenue (Izzati & Najwa,
2018).
2.2 Radio Frequency Identification (RFID)
Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) is an innovative technology with great potential to enhance transparency
and efficiency in supply chains (Desingh et al., 2021). With RFID, every object can have an embedded label
containing a microchip and a radio device emitting signals. This label, often called the "tag," is affixed to each
pallet before shipment from the store. Its purpose is to facilitate the company's identification and monitoring of
each object's status.
RFID technology enables real-time data collection and processing, enhancing the effectiveness of inventory
identification, tracking, and tracing processes within the supply chain. This can improve efficiency and create
sustainable competitive advantages through product quality assurance (Liang et al., 2021). In efforts to enhance
efficiency in business supply chains, companies have opted to adopt RFID technology and provide the latest
training in accounting to support its implementation
With the use of RFID, companies can gain better visibility into production, reduce operational costs, mitigate
losses due to theft, optimize their ever-changing business processes, and accurately record them. Additionally, the

use of RFID technology can enhance inventory management by enabling precise data collection on raw materials
from suppliers, warehouse storage, and distribution of finished products throughout the supply chain. RFID
technology can optimize supply chain logistics by coordinating demand with supply and swiftly identifying
process bottlenecks (Chanchaichujit et al., 2020).
RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is an advancement from barcode technology, offering more
sophisticated capabilities in providing detailed information about a product. Unlike barcodes that require direct
line-of-sight scanning, RFID can be read from a distance without needing direct line-of-sight, enabling faster and
more efficient processing (Kurniawan, 2015; Jules, 2006). RFID technology consists of readers and tags that
identify objects using radio waves. Tags receive information from objects and transmit it to readers. RFID
facilitates the automation of continuous replenishment and vendor-managed inventory. Tags are used to identify
and track objects, while readers gather information from the tags and send it to servers for analysis through
software interfaces. These tags consist of an IC chip and an antenna and go through an assembly process (Unhelkar
et al., 2022). Meanwhile, two frequency types are used in RFID systems: high frequency and low frequency. High
frequency is used for active tags transmitting data over long distances. On the other hand, low frequency is used
for passive tags that can transmit data over short distances due to power obtained from electromagnetic waves
(Tritularsih & Sutopo, 2018). Thus, selecting the appropriate frequency aligns with the RFID system's needs and
applications.
According to Savino et al. (2018), RFID technology appears quite simple in its concept. Information is stored
in RFID tags placed on objects or integrated into smart cards. These tags can be accessed via radio signals, which
compatible RFID readers read. The data read is then displayed on a screen for further use. Subsequently, this data
can be automatically sent to an Information Technology (IT) system for further processing.
2.3 Supply Chain Management (SCM)
Supply chain management is a highly significant business process for every company today. Supply chain
management connects consumer needs with suppliers through a network of channels consisting of various
subprocesses. Therefore, companies need to implement information technology to enhance SCM performance.
Information technology can potentially improve the performance of production distribution systems within the
supply chain (Linda et al., 2022). Tarigan et al. (2021) further explain that supply chain management (SCM) is an
approach aimed at achieving seamless integration and efficiency improvement among various organizations
involved in the supply chain. These organizations include suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, retailers, and
customers. The term "supply chain" encompasses various broad and complex operations involving production and
distribution activities from suppliers and manufacturers to distributors and ultimately reaching end consumers
(Cuadra et al., 2022).
Supply chain management involves six main functions in managing the flow of goods. The first function is
procurement, which involves collecting purchasing data of products and materials from suppliers. The second
function is transportation and logistics, focusing on managing material movement and delivering products to
customers. The third function is production scheduling, which involves creating models to schedule purchases and
production of goods or maintaining safe inventory levels to optimize warehouse capacity, enhance accuracy, and
reduce costs. The fourth function is manufacturing, which involves overseeing the production process, including
estimating production times and ensuring supervision at each stage. The fifth function is quality control, which
involves maintenance and inspection of production facilities to minimize production delays and is typically carried
out by larger companies or factories (Surung et al., 2020).
Effective supply chain management requires improving customer service and operational efficiency within the
supply chain. Good customer service means orders are consistently fulfilled, deliveries are made on time, and there
are minimal product returns. Internal efficiency means companies reap good returns on their investments and find
ways to reduce operational and selling costs. Hugos (2024) in his book states that companies in the supply chain
need to make good and appropriate decisions in five aspects, as follows:
1. Production: Determining the desired products for the market, their quantity, and production timing, and
creating production schedules that consider factory capacity, division of labor, quality control, and
equipment maintenance.
2. Inventory: Deciding on the type and quantity of inventory to be held at each stage of the supply chain to
address uncertainties, considering storage costs, establishing optimal inventory levels, and reorder points.
3. Location: Selecting the most cost-efficient location for production facilities and inventory storage and
deciding whether to use existing facilities or build new ones will determine the product distribution path to
end consumers.

Decisions made in supply chain management regarding these five aspects will determine a company's

management. With technological progress, supply chain management has evolved from manual processes to
complex digital systems-supported processes. Digital supply chains utilize advanced technology to maximize
supply and demand choices and interdependence between them. Digital technology has transformed business
operations, consumer habits, and relationships within the economic sectors. Mobile internet and social networks
contribute to the culture of being always online, hyperconnectivity in the global digital economy, and collecting
vast amounts of user information (Mohsen, 2023).
One significant technological development in supply chain management is implementing Enterprise
Resource Planning (ERP) systems and frequency identification (RFID) technology. This implementation has
become an integral part of technology-driven supply chain management transformation. By adopting ERP and
RFID, companies can enhance visibility and transparency in the supply chain, identify bottlenecks, and respond
to market changes more quickly and accurately. Thus, ERP and RFID implementation become crucial steps in
enhancing efficiency and resilience in supply chain management in this digital era.

Research Method

This study adopts the Systematic Literature Review (SLR) methodology to address the primary research question
concerning the potential improvements in the effectiveness and efficiency of supply chain management through
the implementation of ERP and RFID systems. This rigorous approach is employed to systematically review and
analyze existing literature, providing a comprehensive understanding of the subject matter and facilitating
evidence-based insights into the impact of ERP and RFID systems on supply chain management. This involves
collecting, evaluating, and synthesizing relevant information related to a particular topic or issue from various
literature sources. It follows the five stages or phases Denyer and Tranfield (2009) described in conducting SLR.

the Watase Uake database (a journal search site indexed by Scopus) and Google Scholar, with predetermined

to the research topic. Articles were obtained based on title searches and predetermined keywords. The primary
objective of this literature search is to address the main research question concerning the implementation of ERP
and RFID systems in supply chain management. To ensure a comprehensive response, the research question is
further divided into three sub-research questions: (SRQ 1) Analyzing the implementation of ERP in supply chain
management. (SRQ 2) Analyzing the implementation of RFID in supply chain management. (SRQ 3) Analyzing
the integration of ERP and RFID in supply chain management. Examining these sub-questions enables a detailed
understanding of how ERP and RFID systems are utilized within supply chain management.

Phase 2: Locating the studies
The locating the studies stage is used to find relevant articles through the use of appropriate search databases. In
this study, two databases that provide broad access to literature related to the research statement, namely Watase
Uake and Google Scholar, were used with predefined keywords. The search protocols used to explore the
individual databases in the two databases are the same. For example, for the search in Watase Uake, the search
was applied to the title section, and for Google Scholar, the search also included the title section.
Phase 3: Study Selection and Evaluation
The study selection and evaluation phase is the stage in the research process where the literature that has been
found is chosen and assessed. The objective of this phase is to select relevant and high-quality literature. The
literature that has been selected from the study discovery phase is then read, examined in more detail, and evaluated
based on the predetermined research topic. According to the literature search results, 96 relevant articles were
found. Subsequently, each article was evaluated using predetermined exclusion and inclusion criteria. One of the
criteria employed was the publication period between 2013 and 2023. During this stage, the elimination of articles
that did not meet the criteria was carried out three times. First, duplicate articles between two keywords were
eliminated, resulting in one article. Second, 44 articles published outside the period of 2013–2023 were excluded.
Lastly, two articles that did not have Q1, Q2, Q3, or Q4 rankings in the Scopus database were eliminated.
Following that, a screening stage involved two eliminations. The first elimination was performed manually to
ensure the presence of related keywords in the title and abstract of the article, as a result, 21 articles were excluded
from the analysis due to not meeting the specified criteria. The second elimination was applied to articles that
lacked available data (6 articles). At the final analysis stage, 20 articles were selected that met the criteria and
passed the elimination stage. However, since the number of articles obtained from Watase Uake was limited, 20
additional articles were included from other sources, such as Google Scholar, to achieve this study's desired
number of articles. Consequently, a total of 40 articles were acquired to be utilized in this study.

Phase 4: Analysis and Synthesis
In this phase, the 40 selected articles were analyzed, and the extracted data was synthesized to identify factors and
patterns related to implementing ERP and RFID in supply chain management. This analysis aims to understand
the implementation of ERP and RFID systems in improving the effectiveness and efficiency of SCM processes,
as well as the factors that influence the successful implementation of these two systems in SCM and their
integration to create more efficient and effective SCM. In addition, a bibliometric analysis was conducted to
determine the evolutionary trends related to this topic by paying attention to several important points, such as: (1)
The number of articles published in the last 10 years (2) The distribution of articles by journal database (3) The
distribution of articles by country.
Phase 5: Reporting the Results
The research findings are communicated using tables, statistics, and discussions, following a methodology akin to
that utilized by Toorajipour et al. (2021). This approach encompasses presenting thorough details regarding the
search strategy, criteria for study inclusion or exclusion, selecting relevant studies, quality evaluation, data
extraction, and synthesis of findings.

SRQ 1: The Implementation of ERP Systems in Supply Chain Management (SCM)
Supply chain management and enterprise resource planning (ERP) are considered crucial investments in
corporate information technology, as highlighted by Jenatabadi et al. (2013). ERP systems effectively replace
outdated and incompatible logistics methods by centralizing all information within a single database, as Lukyanova
et al. (2022) noted. Surung et al. (2020) emphasize that ERP encompasses various functional areas, including
Marketing and Sales (M/S), Accounting and Finance (AF), Supply Chain Management (SCM), and Human
Resources (HR). Moreover, the interconnectedness of each department within an ERP system's supply chain
highlights the reliance on preceding links to provide essential information for subsequent links (Sawahir and
Roshan (2021).
There is a critical requirement for information integration across the supply chain and proper enterprise
resource planning. The supply chain assumes a critical role in enhancing the efficiency of managing different
inputs, while enterprise resource planning (ERP) endeavors to enhance resource efficiency as a whole, thereby
reducing production and operational costs (Poranki et al., 2015). Implementing ERP in supply chain management
enables businesses to gain a competitive advantage by improving the flow of process information across functional
systems that connect suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and even end users. This integration facilitates the
seamless exchange of data and enhances overall efficiency throughout the supply chain, leading to improved
competitiveness in the market (Handoko et al., 2015).
ERP has several technologies that are able to integrate with SCM, which helps the supply chain process. One
of the technologies or features in the ERP system is blockchain. The integration between ERP and blockchain is
able to provide positive synergies for the continuity of the supply chain process (SCM). The blockchain feature in
ERP's implementation in SCM can enable users to operate all types of transactions safely and transparently (Hader
et al. 2020). Not only blockchain, ERP systems also have a technology called cloud computing, which, when
applied to SCM, is able to provide the visibility needed for effective decision-making and reduce redundant or
duplicate information systems (Srinivasan and Dey 2013). In ensuring the sustainability of a company, the
integration between ERP and supply chain management can enable companies to more effectively communicate

corporate values and culture to customers as well as suppliers so that companies are able to build sustainable
business relationships (Chandra et al., 2022). Linda et al. (2022) suggest that combining ERP and SCM can affect
organizational performance.
ERP is a breakthrough technology to integrate various fields or divisions within a company to create and
improve effectiveness and efficiency in the company's operational activities. The adoption of ERP into supply
chain management is able to provide positive synergies in the supply chain process by channeling information to
functional systems, which will ultimately be addressed to suppliers and customers. ERP also consists of several
features that can increase the transparency of transactions, increase visibility in decision-making, and minimize
error rates, so its implementation will affect company performance. It is valuable for stakeholders to improve ERP
implementation in each company's business process and execute SCM practices to maintain relationships between
suppliers and customers.
1) ERP implementation success factors in SCM
The successful implementation of an ERP system in a firm aids in providing an overview of the processes
and resources involved in the company's functioning. (Koh et al. 2014). By implementing an ERP system, an
entity is able to improve its overall efficiency and optimize resource utilization. In order to effectively respond
to changes in the business environment, it is crucial for management to ensure seamless integration of all ERP
systems throughout the supply value chain in support of supply chain management (SCM) processes. This
integration enables the real-time flow of data and information, ensuring smooth operations and adaptability to
dynamic market conditions (Almahamid and Hourani 2015). ERP must be fully utilized so that organizations
can feel and benefit from the application of ERP in SCM; therefore, companies must have managers who are
qualified to manage ERP systems (Acar et al. 2017). Before implementing ERP, organizations need to prepare
themselves by building an appropriate technology infrastructure and an organizational structure that supports
a mindset appropriate to the level of organizational change involved (Li et al. 2017).
One of the determining factors for the success of ERP implementation in SCM is also influenced by
management's preparation of competent resources to manage ERP. Where in the process, top management
must have a commitment to hold training sessions arranged for employees, and sufficient funds are allocated
for the implementation process as well as unexpected features that may need to be done (Alawamaleh et al.
2018). Top management commitment is also considered as a significant factor in the successful adoption of
an ERP system. If management considers making their ERP systems and solutions as flexible as possible and
maximizing effectiveness, this will allow the ERP system to expand its benefits to the company or
organization (Trivedi et al. 2018). Thus, the determining factor in the successful implementation of ERP
technology is influenced by how ready management is to transition company activities to an integrated ERP
system. Due to the complexity of ERP implementation, management must prepare human resources competent
in managing ERP for supply chain management. Organizations must also maximize the adoption of ERP
systems by paying attention to the right technological infrastructure so that it will create a system that is also
able to maximize effectiveness in the entire supply chain process.
2) The benefits of ERP implementation in SCM
The implementation of ERP systems in SCM is able to minimize supply chain process delays, increase
efficiency, reduce SCM costs, improve procurement processes, and help organizations predict (Khan et al.
2020). ERP systems will also help organizations set their inventory targets effectively, contribute to better
customizing products and services, and reduce supply chain complexity (Koh et al. 2014). In the research of
Handoko et al. (2015), ERP implementation in SCM is able to minimize errors in the process; by reducing
production costs, companies can gain a competitive advantage in cost leadership and enhance overall company
performance. Not only that, ERP is also able to provide deep insight into business processes so as to provide
information to management as a consideration in decision-making (Pattanayak et al. 2019). This is also
supported by a statement from Ince et al. (2013) that successfully implementing and integrating ERP systems
and SCM ERP practices assist in planning, decision-making, implementation, and enhancing company
performance.
Thus, implementing ERP into the supply chain provides many benefits in improving efficiency and
effectiveness, such as speeding up process times, minimizing costs, and reducing the risk of errors. Not only
does ERP provide benefits in the supply chain process, but it can also provide benefits in decision-making by
interested parties. In general, all the benefits obtained from implementing ERP into SCM will provide positive
synergies for company performance.

SRQ 2: The Implementation of RFID in Supply Chain Management
RFID technology utilizes radio waves to detect and monitor objects equipped with RFID tags (Zhang et al.,
2018). Its distinct characteristics, including the ability to provide unique product identification, seamless
communication, and real-time data availability, significantly contribute to supply chain operations. Through RFID,
precise and efficient tracking of individual items across the entire supply chain becomes possible due to the unique
identification assigned to each product. The seamless communication capability of RFID enables instant and
reliable data exchange between various stakeholders involved in the supply chain network. Moreover, the realtime data availability offered by RFID empowers businesses to make well-timed and well-informed decisions,
optimize inventory management, enhance product visibility, and ultimately enhance overall operational efficiency.
Furthermore, RFID technology facilitates precise inventory management and monitoring processes, increasing
operational efficiency, reducing human errors, and enhancing visibility and transparency throughout the supply
chain (Zhang et al., 2018). Virgilio and Milicchio (2015) found that there are two main approaches to RFID data
processing in the supply chain. The first approach is real-time processing of the data stream. In this approach, data
cleaning techniques such as SMURF adaptive filters address irregularities caused by RFID tag reading
inaccuracies. The second approach involves storing RFID data in a data warehouse and performing
multidimensional analysis. RFID data is collected, compressed, and stored in a data warehouse for more
comprehensive analysis (Virgilio & Milicchio, 2015).
Implementing RFID technology in the supply chain can significantly enhance transaction efficiency and reduce
overall operational costs for companies. Research by Reyes et al. (2015) and Moon (2018) demonstrates these
benefits. One primary advantage of RFID technology is its capacity to identify and track products throughout the
supply chain precisely. According to Savino et al. (2018), installing RFID antennas at various locations or shelves
helps monitor product locations at different stages in the supply chain. This enables companies to gain better
visibility into product movements, streamline inventory control, and boost overall operational efficiency.
Additionally, RFID technology helps mitigate manual errors within the supply chain. Mateen (2019) notes that by
using tags, receivers, and wireless waves, RFID combined with Electronic Product Code (EPC) can reduce human
errors in sectors such as production, distribution, trade, logistics, and security. Integrating RFID technology in the
supply chain can lead to increased transaction cost efficiency, lower operational expenses, improved product

RFID technology in food product supply chain management can also provide benefits. RFID sensors in the

optimization, increased transaction cost efficiency, and reduced overall operational costs—there are some
drawbacks associated with RFID implementation. For instance, RFID tags can be difficult to detect in bright
sunlight due to radio wave interference. Additionally, placing tagged objects too closely can lead to inaccurate
readings. Sidhu et al. (2020) also found that RFID tags can become unreadable when exposed to certain materials
or environmental conditions. Despite these challenges, ongoing advancements in RFID technology are addressing
many of these issues. For example, utilizing different frequencies or incorporating additional technologies like
sensors and improved safeguards can enhance the performance and reliability of RFID systems in supply chain
management.
SRQ 3: The Integration of ERP and RFID in Supply Chain Management
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) is a robust software system designed to integrate and manage various
business functions, including finance, inventory management, production, sales, and human resources. Conversely,
Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) uses radio waves to identify and track items via electronic tags attached to
these objects (Oghazi, 2018). In the modern global market, companies must operate in a highly coordinated and
customized manner to sustain their competitive edge. According to Oghazi (2018), companies engaged in supply
chain collaboration strive to integrate their internal processes and functions to boost their competitive performance.
This integration is intended to streamline operations, enhance efficiency, and optimize overall performance within
the supply chain ecosystem. Essentially, merging ERP and RFID in supply chain management (SCM) provides
companies with improved visibility, better decision-making capabilities, enhanced operational efficiency,
optimized use of technology, and a higher level of accuracy and responsiveness in addressing supply chain
challenges and opportunities.
Along with the passage of time, technological developments have begun to increase, which provide many
benefits to their users, for example, with the existence of the IoT. In their study, Sidhu et al. (2020) highlighted
the continued evolution of the Internet of Things (IoT) over the past few years. The IoT ecosystem has been
enriched with new technologies and protocols that aim to improve accessibility, cost-effectiveness, energy
efficiency, and, most importantly, security. Researchers have provided a conceptual demonstration of integrating
Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) and Wireless Sensor Network (WSN) technologies for an efficient supply
chain management system. In addition, Savino et al. (2018) also found that supply chains with SCOR-integrated
RFID systems allow for improved real-time information sharing between partners, reducing total lead time and
operating costs. Therefore, integrating ERP and RFID in supply chain management (SCM) can contribute to
reducing operational costs and enhancing overall efficiency. With better inventory visibility and accurate
monitoring, companies can avoid unnecessary inventory, reduce high inventory costs, and improve inventory
turnover. In addition, the automation process generated by this integration can also reduce human errors and the
time required in operational processes.
The problem sometimes faced in applying ERP technology is the data input that is still done manually. This
data entry process often experiences delays during data entry, and on the other hand, an inventory check is held at
the finished goods warehouse to adjust the inventory in the system (Tarigan, 2004). This is due to many
possibilities, including the influence of humans, who cannot be expected to perform at a constant data input speed
every time. Thus, the role of a manager is very important in overcoming this. According to the explanation
provided by Zelbst et al. (2019), managers should prioritize the utilization of RFID, IoT, and blockchain
technologies and realize the importance of building integrated and interconnected technology systems to facilitate

The emergence of these technologies can provide good benefits for companies in their supply chains. Oghazi

Simultaneously, ERP systems facilitate the transmission of inventory-related information throughout the entire

Conclusion
This research shows that ERP implementation in SCM has various advantages and plays an important role in
improving the company's efficiency, effectiveness, and overall performance. Integrating technologies such as
blockchain and cloud computing can also provide additional benefits for optimizing SCM processes. In addition,
the application of RFID in SCM can significantly improve traceability, reduce costs, and improve product quality.
Integrating ERP and RFID in SCM provides significant benefits to companies, including more accurate inventory
management, better tracking and management, reduced costs, and improved efficiency. With this integration,
companies can have better visibility into inventory, avoid shortages or excess inventory, and improve inventory
turnover. RFID technology enables high-quality individualized product tracking, while ERP systems enable the
integration of operational and strategic information in the supply chain.

BLOCKCHAIN IN ENTERPRISE RESOURCE PLANNING SYSTEMS:
A COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW OF EMERGING TRENDS, CHALLENGES,
AND FUTURE PERSPECTIVES

Sara Isbaih, Hassan Al Noman, Abdalla Aljarwan, Ibrahim Al Owais, Ahmed Yosry, Zied Bahroun
American University of Sharjah

Abstract:
This research paper conducts a systematic review of the integration of blockchain technology with Enterprise
Resource Planning (ERP) systems, a burgeoning field at the intersection of advanced technology and business
management. The primary objective is to bridge the knowledge gap by synthesizing existing literature, providing insights into the current state, challenges, and potential of blockchain in enhancing ERP systems. The study
follows a structured methodology involving four key stages: Literature Retrieval, Literature Screening, Bibliometric Analysis, and Content Analysis. A comprehensive literature search in the Scopus database resulted in
the identification and analysis of 250 scholarly articles, narrowed down to 66 relevant publications. The findings highlight a significant trend towards decentralized ERP solutions, driven by blockchain's promise of enhanced security, transparency, and efficiency. Key areas impacted by blockchain integration include supply
chain management, accounting, security, traceability, purchasing, and process automation within ERP systems.
The bibliometric analysis reveals a growing academic interest in this domain, with a focus on scalability, data
security, and interoperability. However, the integration faces challenges such as technical complexity, cultural
resistance, and regulatory uncertainties. The study concludes that while blockchain presents transformative
opportunities for ERP systems, realizing this potential requires overcoming substantial technical and organizational hurdles. Future research should concentrate on developing scalable, interoperable blockchain solutions,
tailored to specific industry needs and compliant with regulatory standards, thereby facilitating widespread
adoption and maximizing the benefits of blockchain in ERP systems.
Key words: blockchain technology, enterprise resource planning (erp), supply chain management, pro-

cess automation, data security; smart contracts
INTRODUCTION
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems are integrated software solutions designed to manage and
streamline all core business processes. These systems facilitate the unification of various business functions within
an organization, ensuring seamless data flow and operational efficiency. Typically, ERP systems encompass a
range of modules, each tailored to specific aspects of business management. These modules usually feature a variety of key areas, such as financial and cost accounting,
sales and distribution, materials management, human resources, and production planning, among others [1].
Advancements in technology and evolving business practices have driven substantial evolution of ERP systems.
The emergence of internet and web technologies in the
early 2000s instigated a shift towards more open and flexible ERP architectures, often termed as ERP II [1]. More
recently, the advent of cloud computing has made ERP

systems more scalable, flexible, and cost-efficient, broadening the range of businesses that can utilize ERP functionality [2]. The incorporation of data analytics, artificial
intelligence, and machine learning into ERP systems has
improved decision-making and predictive capabilities,
amplifying their value to contemporary businesses [3].
Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that records
transactions in a secure, transparent, and tamper-evident
manner. The process begins with the submission of a
transaction (TRX), which is then broadcast to a Peer-toPeer (P2P) network. Nodes within this network run a consensus protocol to validate the TRX, ensuring its legitimacy and compliance with the network's rules. Once validated, the TRX is combined with other transactions to
form a new block, which is then appended to the blockchain, creating an indelible link to the previous block. This
sequential linking of blocks forms a chain, and each block
is immutable once added, making the ledger a trusted and

The blockchain technology, was originally designed as a

functions as a decentralized and distributed digital ledger,
recording transactions across multiple computers in a way
that any recorded data cannot be retroactively altered

healthcare, supply chain management, and finance [6].
For instance, in supply chain management, blockchain can
improve traceability, minimize fraud, and enhance contract enforcement [7].
Blockchain's integration with ERP systems promises unparalleled security, transparency, and efficiency. It can enhance data integrity, streamline processes, and improve
auditability [8]. However, despite the promising potential
of blockchain in enhancing ERP systems, practical applications are still in a nascent stage. The integration of blockchain technology into ERP systems represents a promising
area that has yet to be fully explored. Despite the potential of blockchain to enhance data security, transparency,
and efficiency, there is a lack of comprehensive research
synthesizing these scholarly efforts [8]. This research gap
limits our understanding of the implications and benefits
of blockchain integration with ERP systems, potentially
slowing its broader adoption and hindering the realization
of its full potential.
Despite the extensive body of research independently
surrounding both ERP and blockchain technologies, the intersection of these two critical fields remains notably underexplored. While there are sporadic studies that touch
upon the potential synergies and challenges of integrating
blockchain into ERP systems, a noticeable lack exists in
terms of a holistic and integrative review of the collective
research in this sphere [9, 10, 11]. This paper endeavors
to bridge this significant research gap by undertaking a
thorough synthesis and critical analysis of the existing literature pertaining to the amalgamation of blockchain
technology with ERP systems. The objective of this study
is to offer a thorough and detailed insight into the present
situation, identifying areas that have not been extensively
studied and outlining a defined and organized direction
for future academic research. This work is valued not just
academically, but also holds substantial practical value. A
detailed and panoramic view of the current state of research is being offered by this work, positioning it to serve
as a valuable resource for both the academic community
and industry practitioners, guiding future explorations
and applications in this promising and dynamic domain.

METHODOLOGY
The methodology used in this research adheres to a structured and systematic process, meticulously designed to
collect and analyze literature pertaining to the integration
of blockchain technology with ERP systems. This process
is broken down into four essential stages, each playing a
crucial role in ensuring a comprehensive understanding of
the topic. These stages are outlined as follows.
Literature Retrieval
Our research methodology employed a comprehensive
and targeted data collection strategy, leveraging the Scopus database. In this study, we selected the Scopus database for our literature review due to its comprehensive
coverage and extensive range of peer-reviewed journals.
Scopus is recognized for its broad scope across various disciplines, including both well-established and emerging
fields, and offers robust search functionalities. This choice
ensured a thorough and inclusive analysis, aligning with
our aim to capture the most relevant and up-to-date research findings. We meticulously formulated our search
criteria using a combination of specific keywords: "Enterprise Resource Planning Systems" or "ERP", each paired
with terms critical to our study, including “Blockchain”,
"Decentralization", "Cryptocurrency", "Smart payment",
"Smart contract", "Smart report", "Cryptography". This
strategic approach extended beyond mere keyword
matching; we conducted an in-depth search that encompassed titles, abstracts, and keyword fields to unearth potential connections between ERP and blockchain systems.
This thorough search process concluded in the identification and enlistment of an extensive collection of 497
scholarly papers, covering a significant time span from
2018 to 2023. This rich dataset forms the foundation of
our research, offering a diverse range of perspectives and
insights into the integration of blockchain technology with
ERP systems.

widely recognized framework for systematic reviews and
meta-analyses, a systematic literature screening process
was implemented. This facilitated the methodical identification, selection, and evaluation of relevant literature. Initially, 497 papers were retrieved. Upon the elimination of
duplicates, this number was reduced to 305. Each

ERP & Decentralization
ERP systems aim to streamline and optimize business processes, providing a centralized framework for data and resource management. Decentralization, on the other hand,
refers to the distribution of functions and powers away
from a central, authoritative location or group. In the context of blockchain technology, decentralization is a foundational principle. Blockchain enables a distributed ledger
system where data is stored across a network of computers, making it transparent, secure, and resistant to tampering. This decentralization aspect of blockchain is revolutionary for various industries, including ERP systems.
Manceski & Nechkoska [11], highlight the integration of
blockchain into ERP systems as a strategy to develop a decentralized, open-source framework that can dynamically
adapt to the changing needs of businesses, a notable shift
from traditional ERP models. Their focus on the ZERP platform reveals its potential as a groundbreaking, intelligent
ERP solution that harnesses blockchain's distributed
ledger technology for enhanced flexibility, modularity,
and scalability. The cloud-based SaaS architecture of ZERP
is designed to be accessible to a spectrum of business
sizes, from micro-enterprises to large corporations, supporting a diverse network of users and developers. This
new generation of ERP systems aims to be highly collaborative and user-driven, allowing for continuous

ERP & Supply Chain
ERP and blockchain technology are two powerful tools
that can significantly enhance supply chain management.
The supply chain represents the network of all the individuals, organizations, resources, activities, and technologies
involved in the creation and sale of a product, from inception to delivery to the final consumer. Effective supply
chain management is crucial to minimizing waste, reducing costs, and maximizing customer satisfaction, ensuring
that the right goods reach the right place at the right time.
When incorporated into an ERP system managing the supply chain, blockchain technology can significantly boost
the efficiency, traceability, and security of operations. It
offers a real-time, reliable, and verifiable record of each
transaction, enhancing transparency, reducing fraud, and
simplifying administrative tasks. This empowers businesses to optimize their supply chains, reduce costs and
time delays, increase stakeholder trust, and ultimately deliver superior value to customers.
The integration of these two technologies has gained increasing attention due to its potential to revolutionize
supply chain processes and contribute to more efficient,
secure, and transparent operations. This section aims to
explore the latest literature in this domain, discussing the
current advancements, commonalities, differences, and
areas of potential improvement identified by various
scholars.
Sislian & Jaegler [18] discovered that incorporating blockchain into ERP modules can significantly improve supply
chain strategies. This integration not only bolsters the sustainability of corporate performance but also strengthens
supply chain integration. In their 2020 study, Liu et al. [19]
explore the role of blockchain in revolutionizing logistics
enterprises through strategic digital transformation. They
emphasize the critical need for these enterprises to adapt
to the rapidly evolving market dynamics. The authors propose a comprehensive framework that incorporates
blockchain into digital logistics systems. This framework is
designed to significantly enhance operational efficiency
and bolster competitiveness, showcasing blockchain as a
key tool in modernizing and optimizing logistics processes
in the face of technological advancements.
Challenges in adopting blockchain technology are notable. Papathanasiou et al. [20] discuss the reluctance
within the Greek shipping industry towards blockchain
adoption, primarily due to concerns about the potential
exposure of confidential business information, given
blockchain's transparent nature.
On the other hand, some sectors are actively exploring the
advantages of blockchain. For instance, Comuzzi's research in 2018 [21] delves into its implementation in the
ERP system of a Mongolian livestock supply chain, underscoring substantial improvements in transparency and the
reengineering of supply chain processes.

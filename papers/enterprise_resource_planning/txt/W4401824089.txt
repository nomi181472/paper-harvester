Abstract
This article explores the determinants of the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) in small
and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) with special attention to the impact of competencies
and skills. The research was based on data from a representative sample of SMEs in Spain
and used logistic regression econometric analysis. Additionally, the study applied an
innovative AI technique, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to balance the data set.
The findings indicate that SMEs whose business owners / managers have university degrees
or high levels of professional training, those with information technology (IT) experts among
their staff, and those providing IT-related training for employees are all more likely to adopt
AI. Furthermore, SMEs equipped with management skills in Enterprise Resource Planning
(ERP) systems and marketing analytics tools, and SMEs engaged in collaboration with
universities and research centres, demonstrate a greater propensity to integrate AI into their
operations. The implications of these findings are significant for both business management
and public policy. From a managerial perspective, the results underscore the importance of
investing in training programmes and initiatives aimed at upgrading the skill set of employees
and managers to effectively use AI in business operations. On the policy front, the
conclusions suggest an active role for public administrations in promoting the adoption of AI
among SMEs by designing initiatives focused on improving key digital competencies.
Keywords: Artificial intelligence, SME, digital competence, digital skill, digitalisation,
information technology, GAN

Introduction
The application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in business will become a critical factor in the
coming decades for the advancement of productivity and competitiveness of companies
across all sectors (Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson, 2017; Damioli, Van Roy and Vertesy,
2021; Calvino and Fontanelli, 2023). This process of implementation of AI solutions in
companies requires the development of new competencies and skills in their staff and renders
certain previous competences and skills as less relevant or obsolete, since several tasks
previously carried out by humans will be carried out by automatic systems (Acemoglu and
Restrepo, 2019; Felten, Raj and Seamans, 2021; Bodea et al., 2024).
Companies today require managerial teams and employees capable of identifying and
exploiting the potential of AI integration into their businesses (Kietzmann and Pitt, 2020;
Vrontis et al., 2021; Krakowski, Luger and Raisch, 2022; Giraud et al., 2023). Specific
experts in information technology (IT) will be in high demand, although other employees
will also need to develop digital skills (Khatri et al., 2020; Alekseeva et al., 2021; Jaiswal,
Arun and Varma, 2022). However, there is still insufficient evidence on the critical
competencies required for the integration of AI in companies. Therefore, this paper seeks to
address this research question.
The literature on the application of AI has so far concentrated on the case of large companies
that are more proactive in the use of this technology. On the contrary, small and medium
enterprises (SMEs) present significant deficiencies in their digitalisation process as the result
of the obstacles associated with their small scale, which limits their access to financial,
technological, and human resources (OECD, 2021; Calvino and Fontanelli, 2023). In this
sense, there is a specific gap in the literature on the competencies and skills critical to the
adoption of AI in SMEs.
The objective of this paper is to investigate the determinants of the adoption of artificial
intelligence (AI) in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Spain, with special
attention to the impact of competences and skills. Data from a representative sample of
Spanish SMEs, collected through a survey in 2022, was used for analysis. The study uses a
logistic regression model as an econometric technique. Given the low adoption rate of AI
solutions among SMEs in Spain, this paper addresses the associated dataset imbalance by
using a novel AI technique: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).
From a theoretical point of view, this paper contributes to the literature by providing new
insights into the critical role of competencies and skills in the adoption of AI in business and
highlighting the interaction between enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems, marketing
analytics tools, and AI. From a methodological standpoint, this research illustrates the
application of GANs to address issues of dataset imbalances in economics and business
research. From a practical point of view, this article derives relevant implications for
management and public policy, suggesting areas of action to promote the adoption of AI in
small businesses.
The rest of the article is organised as follows. Section 1 reviews the literature on the
application of AI in SMEs and the role of competences and skills, derived from the research
hypotheses for this study. Section 2 describes the data set used and the methodology applied
in the analysis. Section 3 presents and discusses the results of the research. The article
concludes with some final conclusions and implications for practitioners.

1. Review of the scientific literature
The current research landscape reflects a growing interest in the applications of AI in
business, which underlines its transformative potential. Artificial Intelligence offers distinct
advantages to businesses by enhancing effectiveness and objectivity (Raisch and Krakowski,
2021). Its integration spans various functions and tasks within organisations, including that
of marketing (Feng et al., 2021), customer service (Nicolescu and Tudorache, 2022; Xu et
al., 2020), and human resources, particularly in decision-making processes related to hiring
(Khatri et al., 2020; Van den Broek, Sergeeva and Huysman Vrije, 2021). This integration
signifies a collaborative effort between machine intelligence and human input (Herrmann
and Pfeiffer, 2022), and highlights the importance of human involvement in AI applications
(Berente et al., 2021).
The interaction between AI and its applications with both managers and employees is crucial.
Managers play a pivotal role in overseeing AI implementation and ensuring alignment with
organisational goals, while employees contribute domain expertise and feedback, which is
essential for the refinement of AI systems. This symbiotic relationship underscores the
dynamic nature of AI integration in business settings, where human supervision and
collaboration are integral to harnessing its full potential. As the field continues to evolve,
understanding the intricate interplay between AI and human stakeholders becomes
increasingly important for the effective use of AI technologies in business contexts. This
recognition of the complementary roles of AI and human input paves the way for more
nuanced and impactful applications across various organisational functions.
On the one hand, previous research shows that it is necessary for managers to acquire and
enhance new AI skills to sustain their businesses (Krakowski, Luger and Raisch, 2022). In
this regard, AI can demand new qualifications, introduce new tasks, and demand improved
skills (Griva et al., 2021; Vrontis et al., 2021). The literature suggests that managers should
enhance not only their technical competencies, such as understanding AI and its tools, data
visualisation, and analytical knowledge, but also their non-technical competencies, including
empathy, imagination, leadership, and critical thinking (Giraud et al., 2023). Both technical
and non-technical skills are necessary for the application of AI in companies, since technical
skills are valuable for understanding and utilising AI, while non-technical skills are crucial
for the successful implementation of AI (Giraud et al., 2023). The recent literature also
addresses the challenges presented by AI in the context of evolving work methodologies
(Goto, 2022). AI liberates managers from repetitive tasks, thereby enabling them to
concentrate on issues of a more intricate nature (Hagemann, Sünnetcioglu and Stark, 2019).
Consequently, AI can influence administrative skills and can potentially replace tasks such
as information gathering and simple decision-making. However, there remain managerial
skills and tasks that currently cannot be substituted or influenced by AI and its tools. These
include aspects such as imagination and leadership (Giraud et al., 2023), team motivation,
inspiration, and empathy (Plastino and Purdy, 2018), as well as emotional intelligence and
critical thinking (Mattingly and Kraiger, 2019).
On the other hand, in relation to employees, AI and its applications diminish the employee's
responsibilities by handling basic tasks, thus improving efficiency in the firm through an
increased focus of employees on other creative tasks and responsibilities (Acemoglu and
Restrepo, 2018). Moreover, AI enables low-skilled employees to handle complex tasks that
previously might have required specialisation or training (Mayer, Strich and Fiedler, 2020;
Strich, Mayer and Fiedler, 2021). At the same time, while AI facilitates tasks by handling

However, previous studies indicate that managers are the main contributors in the process of
implementing AI and its tools (Sousa and Rocha, 2019; Giraud et al., 2023). In the context
of the AI revolution, managerial proficiency requires the acquisition of not only standard
skills, such as organising, leading, and monitoring, but also a comprehensive understanding
of AI and the synergies between humans and AI (Basu et al., 2022; Herrmann and Pfeiffer,
2022). Therefore, obtaining new and innovative knowledge and skills will require training
programmes and/or relevant university education (Kietzmann and Pitt, 2020; Vrontis et al.,
2021). In the case of SMEs, the personal profile of the business owner/manager plays a
crucial role in the organisation and development of the company (Romero and MartínezRomán, 2012; Martín-Martín, Maya García and Romero, 2022). Companies with business
owners/managers who have a strong educational background may be better positioned to
access the skills necessary to implement AI in their businesses. In addition, today’s top
business schools, such as Oxford Saïd, MIT Sloan, Cambridge Judge, London Business
School, and Harvard Business School, all provide executive education programmes, both
online and in person, for managers and entrepreneurs in the field of AI and related areas.
Therefore, this illustrates the high demand that exists for the development of AI skills. Based
on the aforementioned considerations, this research postulates the following hypothesis:
H1: SMEs whose business owners or managers have a university degree or have completed
an advanced level vocational education and training programme are more likely to implement
AI in their businesses.
In the era of AI, employee skills that incorporate AI can make a significant difference. Prior
studies indicate that digital skills become increasingly important during the implementation
of AI (Oberländer, Beinicke and Bipp, 2019; Carlisle, Ivanov and Dijkmans, 2023).
Additionally, this process requires programming skills, as emphasised in previous research
(Subaveerapandiyan, Paladhi and Maruthaveeran, 2024). Therefore, leveraging IT specialists
within the company can significantly facilitate the digitalisation process, especially when
applying AI. IT specialists are defined, in the context of this research, as employees whose
job is directly related to IT, such as the development, operation, or maintenance of IT systems
or their application (Instituto Nacional de Estadística, 2019). On the basis of these premises,
the following hypothesis is postulated in this research:
H2: SMEs with IT specialists on their staff are more likely to implement AI in their
businesses.
Furthermore, upgrading the skill set of employees remains crucial (Jaiswal, Arun and Varma,
2022). The literature has identified various employee skills relevant to AI and is considered
significant. These include critical thinking abilities (Delanoy and Kasztelnik, 2020),
problem-solving capabilities (Seeber et al., 2020), digital skills (Allmann and Blank, 2021),
soft skills (Hart et al., 2021), and continuous learning capabilities (Jaiswal, Arun and Varma,
2022). In this regard, companies will need to conduct internal training activities or provide

H3: SMEs that conduct IT training activities for their staff are more likely to implement AI

Additionally, companies must reach a certain level of digital maturity to successfully

digitalisation levels between SMEs and large corporations (OECD, 2021). In this context,
collaboration between universities and companies can be regarded as a vital engine driving
technological change and its adoption (Sedlacek, 2013; Wirsich et al., 2016). SMEs can
benefit from collaboration with universities, research centres, and other public infrastructures
to promote digital entrepreneurship and facilitate the digital transformation of existing
companies through the adoption of AI (Chan, Krishnamurthy and Sadreddin, 2022). In this
sense, the following hypothesis is presented:
H5: SMEs that collaborate with universities and research centres on digitalisation-related
issues are more likely to implement AI in their businesses.

2. Research methodology
In this section, the data used in this research are first presented, followed by an explanation
of other aspects of the methodology employed.
2.1. Data description
Data for this research were obtained from a survey conducted in the second quarter of 2022
on Spanish SMEs with at least one employee and up to 200 employees. Small and mediumsized enterprises participating in the survey were randomly selected from the Iberian Balance
Sheet Analysis System (SABI) database. Our stratified sample is representative of the SME
population in Spain with an error of ±5.0% at a confidence level of 95%, by applying simple
random sampling and considering that the population is binomial (p = q = 0.5, the most
unfavourable situation). The survey technique employed was that of computer-assisted
telephone interviewing (CATI). In the fieldwork, there was a response rate of 21.5%. No
relevant bias was detected between respondents and non-respondents. The final data set for
this analysis is made up of 836 valid observations.

The initial data set comprised 54 variables. Through a rigorous selection process involving
experts and the application of suitable statistical techniques to identify the most relevant
variables pertaining to the endogenous variable that concerns the use of AI in SMEs, a subset
of 10 exogenous variables has been chosen to elucidate the dependent variable.
The dependent variable in this study (AI) is a binary variable that captures whether, in the
three previous years (2019-21), the company had used AI techniques, such as automatic
learning and machine learning, processing and/or generation of natural language, voice
recognition, and other methods for the analysis of big data.
Regarding the explanatory variables, we first consider the following set of variables
associated with competencies within the company:
 Entrepreneurs/managers with university degrees or higher professional studies (Ent_edu):
This binary variable captures those SMEs with entrepreneurs/managers who hold a
university degree or higher professional training.
 Medium-level staff with IT expertise (IT_exp): This binary variable takes the value 1 for
companies that have IT experts in medium-level positions (0 otherwise).
 IT training initiatives (IT_train): This binary variable indicates whether the company had
carried out training activities to improve the IT skills of its employees within the three years
prior to the survey.
 ERP Systems (ERP): This binary variable indicates whether companies have implemented
ERP systems.
 Marketing Analytics Tools (Mark_analy): This binary variable indicates whether
companies have implemented digital marketing analytics tools.
 Cooperation with universities and R&D centres (Coop_univ). This binary variable captures
whether SMEs cooperate with universities and research centres on aspects related to
digitalisation.
Firm size (Size), in terms of the number of employees, the age of the company (Age), and
dummy variables for sectors (Trade or Services) are also considered in the analysis as control
variables.

2.2. Generative Adversarial Networks
In our database, only 85 SMEs have employed artificial intelligence techniques, which
represent approximately 10% of the total number of observations. This level of application
is consistent with other studies on the adoption of AI in SMEs in Spain (ONTSI, 2023).
However, alongside these 85 enterprises that have utilised AI, our data set includes 751 other
enterprises that have not used AI, which results in a clear imbalance. This imbalance issue is
addressed in this paper through the use of GANs (Goodfellow et al., 2014; Goodfellow et al.,
2020), an emerging technique for both semi-supervised and unsupervised learning (Radford,
Metz and Chintala, 2015; Donahue, McAuley and Puckette, 2018; Liu et al., 2018).

The application of GANs in data augmentation has shown notable results on the performance
of applied predictive models (Douzas and Bação, 2018; Mottini, Lheritier and Acuna-Agost,
2018; Ding, 2019). In the field of social sciences, it is still an emerging technique, but there
are several studies in which GANs have achieved satisfactory results and improvements over
other methods. Applications that have demonstrated the efficiency of GANs in various
economic issues include the detection of banking fraud (Langevin et al., 2022), detection of
trading strategies (Koshiyama, Firoozye and Treleaven, 2020), asset price generation (Han,
Ma and Zhang, 2022), generation of mobility networks (Mauro et al., 2022), economic
scenario generators for market risk assessment (Wang et al., 2024), generation of financial
time series (Vuletić, Prenzel and Cucuringu, 2024) or ensuring customer privacy (Anand and
Lee, 2023), among others.
These studies support the utility of GANs in economic research, given their ability to address
various challenges, including data scarcity, imbalance, missing data, and lack of responses
in surveys, thereby producing superior results compared to conventional methods. This is the
motivation to employ GANs in this article, where they are used to generate synthetic data to
balance the data set.
Generative Adversarial Networks comprise two key components – the Generator and the
Discriminator – that operate in an interplay. Initially, the Generator takes in a random noise
vector and produces synthetic data. Subsequently, these synthetic data are amalgamated with
authentic data to train the Discriminator. The Discriminator´s pivotal task is to distinguish
between real and synthetic data. The Generator strives to produce synthetic data
convincingly, aiming to deceive the Discriminator, while the Discriminator endeavours not
to be misled. This dynamic adversarial process, elucidated by Salimans et al. (2016), Heusel
et al. (2017), Ge et al. (2018), and Bynagari (2019), propels the Generator towards enhancing
its capability in generating synthetic data that closely mirror authentic data.
The primary objective of this endeavour lies in the replication and validation of original
company data. This requires the construction of a model that is capable of faithfully
mimicking real-world data. The comprehensive formula, outlined by Gonzalez-Abril et al.
(2022), which integrates both the Discriminator and Generator, is as follows:

To apply GANs, observations that exhibit the target characteristic of interest, namely SMEs
that have adopted AI solutions, were selected from the database, resulting in 85 observations.
The Generator’s structure, denoted as G(z), comprises six dense layers. It takes the noise
vector as input and produces an output vector of size 10, corresponding to the number of
features. On the other hand, the Discriminator’s architecture, labelled as D(z), consists of
three dense layers, each incorporating batch normalisation and leaky (alpha = 0.01) layers.
The Generator and Discriminator both employ the Adam optimiser with a uniform learning
rate set at 0.0001. The Discriminator utilises cross-entropy loss, tailored for its binary
classification task. In contrast, the Generator’s loss function relies on mean square error,
calculated from the percentiles disparity between the real and synthetic data. This specific
loss function is chosen because of its capability to empirically enhance the goodness-of-fit
performance of the real and synthetic distributions by adjusting the number of percentiles
appropriately.
The dataset undergoes validation before its utilisation. Goodness-of-fit tests are conducted,
with the null hypothesis (H0) positing that the set of synthetic enterprises originates from the
same distribution as the real enterprises, at a significance level of 5%. Pearson’s chi-squared
tests are used for categorical variables and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for quantitative

Regarding the explanatory variables, they are all significant and have a positive sign. These
findings provide strong evidence to support all hypotheses postulated in Section 2.
First, our results indicate that SMEs whose business owners/managers have a university
degree or high professional training are more likely to adopt AI in their businesses, thus
supporting hypothesis H1. This finding underscores the importance of acquiring new and
innovative knowledge and skills for entrepreneurs/managers, which can be obtained through
university education or training. Entrepreneurs with a strong educational background are
better equipped to understand the potential associated with AI adoption and to successfully
implement the necessary changes in their companies. This importance of the education level
of business owners/managers aligns with previous evidence from studies by Romero and
Martinez-Roman (2012) and Martín-Martín, Maya García and Romero (2022).
Similarly, the presence of technical staff with IT expertise and IT training initiatives for
employees is observed to exert a strong positive effect on AI adoption, supporting our
hypotheses H2 and H3. Companies with IT experts among their staff and those conducting
IT training activities exhibit a more positive attitude towards AI adoption and possess the
necessary skills to facilitate successful implementation of this technology. This suggests that
such companies have greater readiness or capability for AI adoption and highlights the

importance of SMEs prioritising skilled personnel and investing in continuous training to
effectively leverage advanced technologies such as AI. This finding aligns with previous
literature that emphasises the importance of skills and competencies for AI adoption
(Alekseeva et al., 2021; Calvino and Fontanelli, 2023).
In particular, our results indicate that SMEs that use marketing analytics tools and ERP

more advanced digital skills and higher digital maturity are better positioned to understand

it is not advisable to jump into the void when seeking to maximise the probabilities of
success. The application of advanced tools for digital management indicates that SMEs
already possess the necessary competencies and skills for adopting AI. Additionally, these
tools serve as a powerful source for obtaining and structuring large amounts of data, which
are necessary for training AI models. This finding is in line with previous analyses that point
out the potential interaction between ERP systems, marketing analytics, and AI (Petrescu and
Krishen, 2023; Jawad and Balázs, 2024).
Lastly, our results reveal that cooperation with universities and research centres exerts a
strong positive effect on the implementation of AI in SMEs, which supports hypothesis H5.
Collaboration with universities and research centres facilitates the implementation of AI in
SMEs by providing access to the technological and knowledge resources that are critical to
the implementation of AI in business. This finding is consistent with Chan, Krishnamurthy
and Sadreddin (2022), where SMEs are shown to benefit from the establishment of
collaboration with universities and research centres to facilitate their digital transformation.
Moreover, the effect associated with this variable is observed to be the greatest based on the
odds ratios (see Exp(B) in Table no. 3).
In general, these results underscore the importance of competencies and skills in influencing
the adoption of AI within SMEs in Spain. The findings elucidate how digital human capital
directly impacts the adoption of AI tools in business.

Conclusions
This article delves into the evolving landscape of competency requirements spurred by AI
adoption within companies and deals particularly in the case of SMEs in Spain. In doing so,
we highlight the pivotal role of upgrading skill sets, adaptability, and strategic workforce
planning to harness the potential of AI while ensuring sustainable growth and
competitiveness in today’s dynamic business environment.
The approach of SMEs to digitalisation is influenced by the specific characteristics of these
types of companies, which can entail certain limitations to adopting AI in their businesses.
In this context, our paper highlights some critical competencies and skills that favour the
adoption of AI in SMEs. To our knowledge, this study represents the first attempt to examine
the competency requirements for AI implementation with a specific focus on SMEs. By
empirically investigating the factors associated with AI adoption in SMEs in Spain, our
research therefore fills a relevant gap in the existing literature. Our findings not only provide

From a methodological standpoint, our study employs an innovative technique known as

such as medicine and engineering, and has only recently been adopted in the realm of

Our analysis reveals that SMEs with business owners/managers holding a university degree
or high-professional training, those with IT experts on their staff, and those conducting ITrelated training for their employees are more inclined to adopt AI technologies. Furthermore,
SMEs equipped with advanced digital management skills (using ERP systems and marketing
analytics tools) and engaged in collaboration with universities and research centres exhibit a
higher propensity for the integration of AI into their operations.
These findings have significant implications for both business management and public
policy. From a managerial point of view, the results underscore the critical need for the
enhancement of digital skills within companies to facilitate the successful adoption of AI
technologies. In this sense, the hiring of IT specialists is crucial for SMEs to adopt AI. They
bring expertise in assessing needs, integrating systems, managing data, and ensuring
cybersecurity. Their skills are essential for successful AI implementation, driving innovation
and efficiency in SMEs. Furthermore, the results presented in this document highlight the
importance of investing in training programmes and initiatives aimed at improving the skills
of employees and managers to effectively leverage AI in business operations.
In particular, this research shows that implementing ERP systems and marketing analytics in
SMEs can significantly favour the adoption of AI in business operations. ERP systems
streamline and integrate various business processes, providing a centralised platform to
manage resources, finances, and operations. This integration results in a wealth of structured
data, which is essential for training AI models. Additionally, marketing analytics tools allow
SMEs to gather, analyse, and interpret vast amounts of customer data, leading to better
insights and more informed decision making. Together, these technologies create a robust
data infrastructure, improving the capacity for AI implementation. Using data from ERP
systems and marketing analytics, AI can automate and optimise tasks, predict trends,
personalise customer interactions, and improve overall efficiency. Finally, SMEs should also
strive to establish closer collaboration with universities and research centres to support their
AI implementation projects within their businesses. These cooperative relationships can
facilitate SMEs’ access to external knowledge, skills, and capabilities that are not available
within the organisation.
On the policy front, public administrations can play a crucial role in fostering AI adoption
among SMEs by designing initiatives focused on improving key digital competencies. This
could involve developing targeted training programmes, providing financial incentives or
grants for technology adoption, and facilitating collaboration between SMEs and educational
institutions or research centres. Furthermore, the development of AI competences within
education systems is strategically important (Grădinaru et al., 2024). By actively promoting
the development of digital skills and capabilities within SMEs, policymakers can contribute
to driving innovation, productivity, and competitiveness in the broader economy.
This research is not without limitations. In this sense, it should be noted that most of the
variables used in the econometric analysis are dichotomous. Future research could use Likert

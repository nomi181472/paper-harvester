Queue-Aware Token Communications: Bridging AI
Representation and Wireless Service

arXiv:2610.16730v1 [cs.AI] 12 Oct 2026

Yuquan Xiao, Qinghe Du, Chaoyang Zhang, Wenchi Cheng, Jingqing Wang, Tao Chen, and Jiangzhou Wang
Abstract—The rapid advancement of foundation models and
edge intelligence is transforming wireless networks from dataoriented communication systems into artiﬁcial intelligence (AI)native service platforms. Token communications (TokenCom) has
emerged as a promising paradigm that directly transmits taskoriented AI representations. However, existing studies typically
assume that generated tokens can be transmitted immediately,
overlooking the queueing dynamics arising from the mismatch
between token workload generation and time-varying wireless
service capability. To address this limitation, we introduce QueueAware TokenCom, which explicitly incorporates token queues
into the AI service pipeline. We present an end-to-end architecture integrating AI representation generation, token workload
management, wireless transmission, and downstream task inference. We then discuss two key design directions: budget-aware
token representation optimization and queue-aware token service
optimization, with the token budget serving as a critical interface
between AI performance and communication efﬁciency. Moreover, we investigate ﬁxed and adaptive token budget strategies to
characterize the fundamental tradeoff between AI performance
and communication efﬁciency in dynamic wireless environments.
Finally, we outline research directions toward closed-loop, taskaware, and scalable TokenCom systems.
Index Terms—Token communications, token budget, token
queue model.

I. I NTRODUCTION
The evolution of artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) is reshaping
the role of future wireless networks from merely transporting
information to supporting intelligent services [1]. Traditional
communication systems are primarily designed for bit-level
information transmission, where the fundamental objective is
to reliably and efﬁciently deliver raw data. However, with
the rapid development of foundation models, multimodal AI,
embodied intelligence, and edge intelligence, wireless networks are increasingly expected to support AI inference tasks
with stringent requirements on latency, reliability, and efﬁciency [2], [3]. Different from conventional data transmission,
AI tasks usually focus on extracting task-relevant information rather than perfectly recovering the original data. This
paradigm shift indicates that future communication systems
should move beyond transmitting raw data toward delivering
task-oriented AI representations. In this context, tokens, which
serve as fundamental information units in modern AI models,
are emerging as promising communication objects for AInative networks.

Recent advances in Transformer-based models have established tokens as a key interface between input data and AI
inference [4]. By mapping raw data into compact token representations, modern AI models can effectively capture semantic
and task-relevant features for downstream inference. Therefore, token communications (TokenCom) has been proposed
as a new communication paradigm, where task-oriented token
representations are directly transmitted instead of complete
raw data [5]–[7]. Existing studies mainly focus on reducing
token redundancy and communication overhead while preserving AI task performance [8], [9]. Representative approaches
include token selection and token merging for reducing the
number of transmitted tokens, as well as feature compression
and quantization for reducing token payloads [10], [11]. These
efforts demonstrate the potential of token-based transmission
for improving the efﬁciency of AI services.
Despite these advantages, existing studies mainly focus
on token representation optimization while assuming that
generated tokens can be immediately transmitted in a timely
manner. Such an assumption may not hold in some practical AI
services, where continuous perception, real-time monitoring,
and robotic sensing applications continuously generate token
workloads [12], while wireless transmission capability dynamically ﬂuctuates due to channel fading, user mobility, and
interference. As a result, the token workload arrival process
and wireless service capability may become mismatched, leading to accumulated token workloads and additional queueing
latency.
To address this limitation, we introduce Queue-aware TokenCom, which jointly considers token representation design
and token queue management. By explicitly modeling tokens
as communication workloads, Queue-aware TokenCom aims
to achieve a better balance between AI task performance
and communication service quality under dynamic wireless
environments. Following this, the main contribution of this
article are summarized as follows: 1) we propose an endto-end architecture for Queue-aware TokenCom, which establishes a uniﬁed pipeline connecting AI representations,
token workloads, token queues, and wireless services; 2)
we identify the design principles of this emerging paradigm
and discuss two fundamental optimization directions: budgetaware token representation optimization and queue-aware token service optimization; 3) we investigate the accuracy-delay
tradeoff under ﬁxed and adaptive token budget strategies in
dynamic wireless environments; and 4) we highlight several
future research directions, including closed-loop TokenCom
optimization, task-aware and risk-sensitive TokenCom, and
scalable multi-agent TokenCom networks.

B. Token Queue Model

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the architecture of Queue-aware TokenCom consists of four key components: the transmitter-side
submodel, token queue, wireless transmission, and receiverside submodel. The input data are ﬁrst preprocessed, e.g.,
tokenization and embedding, and then transformed by the
transmitter-side submodel, in which task-relevant token representations are ﬁrst extracted for subsequent AI inference.
After that, since wireless resources are inherently limited,
directly transmitting all generated tokens may introduce excessive communication overhead and lead to severe queueing
congestion, and thus a token compression module is employed
to adapt the generated token workload by reducing the number,
dimension, or precision of transmitted tokens. Note that the
representation and compression modules can be also tightly
coupled. Various compression strategies, such as token selection and token merging, can be integrated into the transmitterside submodel to achieve an appropriate balance between task
performance and communication efﬁciency.
The compressed tokens then enter the token queue before
wireless transmission. Different from existing TokenCom studies that mainly focus on reducing token redundancy to directly
adapt to wireless channel, Queue-aware TokenCom explicitly
considers the temporal mismatch between token workload and
the transmission capability of wireless channels. In some AI
services, token workloads are continuously generated, whereas
wireless transmission capability ﬂuctuates due to channel
variations, mobility, and interference. Therefore, token queue

workload and wireless service capability, the token queue
evolution can be modeled as

where Q(t) denotes the accumulated token workload waiting
for transmission, A(t) represents the arriving token workload
generated by AI tasks, and S(t) denotes the token service
capability provided by the wireless transmission module. This
queue evolution captures the mismatch between token workload generation and wireless transmission capability. When
the arriving token workload exceeds the available service
capability, the queue length increases and results in additional
queueing latency; otherwise, the accumulated workload can be
gradually cleared.
A key feature of the proposed architecture is the Queueaware control loop. As shown in Fig. 1, the queue control
policy observes both queue state and channel state, and dynamically adjusts the token budget or transmission strategy. Specifically, token budget serves as a fundamental control variable
that determines how much information can be preserved in the
transmitted token representations, while simultaneously affecting the communication workload entering the token queue.
Depending on the adopted token compression mechanism,
the budget can be characterized from different perspectives,
including the number of transmitted tokens, feature dimension,
representation precision, or overall payload size.

C. Performance Evaluation Metrics
Since the system aims to simultaneously deliver high-quality
AI inference and efﬁcient wireless service, two categories of
metrics are considered: AI performance metrics that evaluate
the quality of AI inference, and communication performance
metrics that quantify the efﬁciency and timeliness of token
delivery. On the one hand, one main objective of TokenCom is
to preserve sufﬁcient information for subsequent AI inference.
Therefore, AI performance metrics depend on the speciﬁc AI
applications. For example, image classiﬁcation tasks can be
evaluated by accuracy, object detection tasks can be measured
by mean average precision (mAP), while other AI services
may adopt task success rate or semantic similarity as performance indicators. These metrics reﬂect how effectively the
transmitted token representations support the target AI tasks.
On the other hand, the token queue performance characterizes
whether the generated token workload can be efﬁciently served
under dynamic wireless conditions. Among various queuerelated metrics, average queueing delay is a critical indicator
for real-time AI services, as it directly reﬂects the waiting time
before tokens are delivered for inference. In addition, queue
length can be used to characterize workload accumulation,
while deadline violation probability can evaluate the reliability
of latency-sensitive AI applications.

A. Budget-aware Token Representation Optimization
Token representation optimization aims to maximize AI task
utility under communication budget constraints. Since token
budget determines the amount of information that can be
delivered through wireless networks, two key design problems
need to be addressed: how to compress token representations
into a given budget and how to train AI models to adapt to
different budget conditions.
1) Token-to-Budget Compression: Given a target token budget, the objective of token-to-budget compression is to reduce
the token workload while preserving sufﬁcient information
for downstream AI inference. Various compression strategies
can be adopted to achieve token-to-budget adaptation. One
category is token number reduction, including token selection
and token merging. Token selection removes tokens with
limited contribution to the target task, while token merging
aggregates redundant tokens into compact representations.
Another category is token payload reduction, where the feature
dimension or representation precision of each token is reduced
through feature compression or quantization. Although these
approaches differ in implementation, they share the same
objective: reducing communication overhead under a given
token budget while maximizing the retained task utility.
2) Budget-aware TokenCom Learning: The compression
strategy determines the transmitted token workload, while
the AI model determines how effectively such workload can
support the target task. Therefore, the transmitter-side submodel, including the compression module, and the receiverside submodel should be jointly optimized in an end-to-end
manner under the target token budget conditions. As illustrated
in Fig. 2, the token budget distribution plays a critical role in
training budget-aware TokenCom models. During training, the

B. Queue-aware Token Service Optimization
While token representation optimization determines the
amount and quality of token workload entering the communication system, queue-aware token service optimization focuses
on how to efﬁciently deliver such workload under dynamic
wireless conditions. The evolution of the token queue is jointly
determined by the token workload arrival process and the
wireless service process. Speciﬁcally, the token arrival process
is affected by multiple factors, including task arrival patterns,
token budget allocation, and adopted compression strategies.
In contrast, the token service capability is determined by
the wireless transmission environment and resource allocation
strategies. The mismatch between token workload arrival and
wireless service capability leads to dynamic queue evolution,
making queue-aware control essential for maintaining efﬁcient
AI service delivery. To efﬁciently serve token workloads,

various measures can be conducted by exploiting both AI
task characteristics and wireless network dynamics from three
aspects:
1) Adaptive Token Budget Control: Adaptive token budget
control provides a direct mechanism to regulate the workload
entering the queue. By exploiting real-time network states,
such as queue length and channel quality, the token budget
or compression level can be dynamically adjusted. When the
queue pressure increases, more aggressive token compression
can reduce workload arrival, while better communication conditions can allow richer token representations to improve AI
task performance. Such adaptive control enables a dynamic
balance between representation quality and queueing performance.
2) Token Scheduling Priority: Token scheduling determines
the service priority among queued tokens. Tokens may have
different contributions to the ﬁnal AI task, and thus scheduling
policies can consider semantic importance, task urgency, or
inference deadlines to prioritize more valuable tokens and improve overall task performance under limited communication
resources.
3) Wireless Resource Allocation: Wireless resource allocation optimizes the transmission capability for token delivery.
By jointly considering queue states and channel conditions,
communication resources, such as bandwidth and transmit
power, can be dynamically allocated to balance queueing delay
and transmission efﬁciency [14]. For example, more resources
can be assigned when the queue accumulates signiﬁcant token
workload or when urgent AI tasks require timely delivery.
Note that due to the adaptive token budget control, the
amount of data to be transmitted varies across tasks. Then, it
becomes challenging to characterize the task-level communication delay based on a time-indexed queueing process, since
the relationship between the token backlog and the number
of unﬁnished tasks is no longer straightforward. Therefore,
as illustrated in Fig. 3, a task-indexed modeling approach
can be adopted to characterize the communication delay of
individual tasks and facilitate its minimization through wireless resource allocation optimization. The entire optimization
processes reveal that token representation and queue service
are inherently coupled rather than independent. On the one
hand, the queue service policy determines the required token budget and compression behavior, thereby inﬂuencing
the budget distribution experienced during deployment. On
the other hand, the resulting AI task performance further
affects the optimal communication strategy, since different
task requirements may lead to different workload generation
and service preferences. Therefore, Queue-aware TokenCom
should be viewed as a bidirectional AI-communication system,
where token representation optimization and Queue-aware
service optimization continuously interact with each other.

A. Learnable Top-K Token Selection Module
The token selection module aims to identify the most informative tokens under a given token budget. Instead of using
manually designed token importance criteria, we introduce a
lightweight learnable scoring network to adaptively evaluate
the contribution of each patch token. As shown in Fig. 4,
after the third transformer layer, each patch token is ﬁrst
normalized and concatenated with the normalized token budget
K/Kmax , where Kmax = 197. The resulting feature is fed
into a shared shallow multi-layer perceptron network, which
outputs an importance score for each patch token. Since all
tokens share the same scoring network, the module can be
efﬁciently implemented through parallel computation while
introducing only a negligible number of additional parameters.

Since the CLS token is always preserved, the adaptive TopK module selects K − 1 patch tokens from the 196 patch
tokens. Given the obtained scores, the K − 1 patch tokens
with the highest scores are selected together with the CLS
token. Note that the hard Top-K operation is inherently
non-differentiable and prevents direct gradient propagation.
To enable end-to-end training, we adopt a NeuralSort-style
continuous approximation combined with a straight-through
estimator. Speciﬁcally, the forward pass uses the exact hard
Top-K selection to guarantee the desired transmission budget,
while the backward pass employs a soft permutation surrogate
to propagate gradients through the token scoring network.
Therefore, the entire transmitter, token selection module, and
receiver can be jointly optimized using the ﬁnal classiﬁcation
loss.
B. Fixed and Adaptive Token Budget Strategies
As discussed previously, the token budget becomes a critical
control variable: a larger K preserves more task-relevant
information but introduces heavier communication workload
and potentially larger queueing latency. We consider two
token budget strategies to investigate the impact of workload
adaptation.
1) Fixed Token Budget Strategy: With this strategy, each
task is transmitted using a predeﬁned number of tokens. Such
a strategy provides a stable operating point and allows the AI
model to be speciﬁcally optimized for a given token budget. In
addition, this strategy ensures that the incoming data volume
remains constant for each image classiﬁcation task, thereby
simplifying the task-level queueing latency modeling. However, it cannot exploit temporal variations of wireless channels.
Consequently, when the selected token workload approaches
or exceeds the average service capability, accumulated tokens
may cause rapidly increasing task completion delay.
2) Adaptive Token Budget Strategy: The key idea of this
strategy is to exploit channel predictability and dynamically
match token workload with future service capability. At each
task arrival instant, the controller observes the current token
service capability µ(t) and the queue length Q(t). Here, we
propose an adaptive token budget strategy for highly timecorrelated wireless channels, where the slot duration is set to

6

10 ms and the channel correlation coefﬁcient is set to ρ =
0.99. This setting is reasonable for the uplink transmission of
a ﬁxed-position camera, where the channel generally exhibits
high temporal correlation at such a short time scale. The arrival
interval of image tasks is set to M = 10 time slots. Since
the channel is highly correlated, the future service capability
within two adjacent arrival intervals can be approximately predicted according to the current channel condition. Therefore,
the available service opportunity before the next task arrival
is estimated as Ĉ(t) = M µ(t). The adaptive token budget is
then determined as Kraw (t) = max{1, α(Ĉ(t) − Q(t)), where
α controls the aggressiveness of workload injection. A larger
value of α allows richer token representations but introduces
higher queueing risk, while a smaller value of α adopts
a more conservative strategy. Finally, Kraw (t) is projected
onto the feasible budget set Kset = {1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100}.
This adaptive strategy enables the system to allocate more
tokens under favorable wireless conditions and reduce token
workload during potential congestion periods. Accordingly,
the AI model should be trained based on the token budget
distribution generated by this adaptive strategy.
To summarize, ﬁxed token budget strategies provide simplicity and predictable behavior, and they allow the AI model
to be speciﬁcally optimized for a single operating point
without parameter sharing among different budgets. In contrast, adaptive token budget strategies require the model to
support dynamically varying token budgets, which introduces
additional training complexity and potential performance interference due to shared model parameters. However, adaptive
strategy provides greater ﬂexibility under dynamic wireless
environments by jointly considering channel conditions and
queue states.

Communication Delay

increase. This observation indicates that pursuing marginal
AI performance improvement with excessive token budget
may trigger severe queueing penalties, especially when the
workload approaches the wireless service boundary. Next, we

the adaptive policy dynamically adjusts the token workload
according to the predicted available capacity and queue state.

V. P OTENTIAL R ESEARCH D IRECTIONS
Although Queue-aware TokenCom provides a new perspective for integrating AI representation and wireless service,

7

several fundamental challenges remain open. In this article,
we mainly focus on a single-user scenario and characterize the
tradeoff between AI task performance and queue-level service
efﬁciency. However, future AI-native networks are expected
to involve dynamic interactions, heterogeneous AI tasks, and
multiple intelligent agents. These emerging requirements motivate further investigation into more intelligent, adaptive, and
scalable Queue-aware TokenCom systems.
A. Closed-Loop TokenCom Optimization
Queue-aware TokenCom discussed in this article mainly
follows a sequential design paradigm, where communication
policies determine token budgets and AI models are subsequently optimized to adapt to the resulting token distributions.
However, token representation quality and communication
service capability are inherently coupled: the optimal token
budget depends on AI task requirements and queue states,
while the generated token workload further affects future
communication decisions. Therefore, future research should
investigate closed-loop TokenCom optimization, where AI
models, token generation strategies, and queue control policies
can continuously interact and adapt according to dynamic task
requirements and wireless environments. Such a framework
can enable jointly optimized token representation and communication strategies, rather than independently designing them.
B. Task-aware and Risk-Sensitive TokenCom
Future AI services would exhibit highly heterogeneous
requirements. For example, autonomous systems and safetycritical applications require not only low latency but also
stringent reliability guarantees on task completion. Therefore,
future research should investigate task-aware and risk-sensitive
TokenCom, where token generation, budget allocation, and
queue management are jointly optimized according to the
speciﬁc requirements of different AI tasks. In particular, new
performance metrics beyond average latency, such as deadline
violation probability and task failure probability, should be
incorporated to support reliable AI services.
C. Scalable Multi-Agent TokenCom Networks
As an emerging AI-native communication paradigm, Queueaware TokenCom is currently formulated mainly for singleuser scenarios, where the token workload generation and queue
dynamics are relatively easier to characterize. However, future
AI-native networks are expected to support a large number of
intelligent agents, such as autonomous robots, vehicles, and
embodied AI systems, which collaboratively perform complex
tasks through wireless networks. In such multi-agent scenarios,
each agent may generate heterogeneous token workloads with
different semantic importance, task objectives, and latency
requirements. Therefore, scalable Multi-Agent TokenCom requires new task-aware communication mechanisms beyond
conventional multi-user scheduling. Future research should
investigate intelligent token scheduling, cooperative queue
management, and resource allocation strategies that jointly
consider task utility, semantic importance, and latency requirements. Moreover, exploiting semantic relationships among
agents to enable collaborative token exchange and distributed

AI inference represents an important direction toward future
AI-native networks.
VI. C ONCLUSION
In this article, we introduced Queue-aware TokenCom, a
new communication paradigm that treats AI tokens as communication workloads and jointly considers token representation,
workload management, and wireless service delivery. By incorporating token queues into the AI communication pipeline,
the proposed perspective reveals the intrinsic coupling between
AI task performance and communication efﬁciency, where token budget serves as a critical interface connecting AI models
and wireless systems. We further discussed key design principles, including token representation optimization and Queueaware token service optimization, and highlighted future research directions toward closed-loop TokenCom optimization,
task-aware and risk-sensitive AI services, and scalable multiagent TokenCom networks. We envision that Queue-aware
TokenCom will provide a promising framework for future
AI-native networks, where communication systems can intelligently manage AI workloads by jointly optimizing token
generation, queue dynamics, and wireless service delivery.

Semantic Edge-Cloud Communication for Real-Time
Urban Traffic Digital Twins

arXiv:2610.14187v1 [cs.NI] 9 Oct 2026

Murat Arda Onsu1 , Arman Dogru1 , Poonam Lohan1 , Burak Kantarci1 , Aisha Syed2 ,
Matthew Andrews2 , and Sean Kennedy2
Abstract—Urban traffic systems are becoming increasingly
complex, demanding real-time traffic perception, proactive collision prediction, and intelligent management to improve safety
and reduce congestion. Although AI-driven traffic management
has shown strong promise, its success relies on diverse, highquality data spanning critical scenarios such as collisions, nearcollisions, and traffic rule violations. Digital twins offer a powerful
solution by enabling synchronized virtual replicas of physical
traffic environments for real-time monitoring, collision prediction,
traffic-flow optimization, and diverse realistic scenario generation. However, continuously synchronizing a high-fidelity urban
traffic digital twin through edge surveillance cameras requires
substantial edge-cloud communication, and transmitting raw
video streams leads to severe bandwidth and latency constraints.
To overcome this challenge, this paper proposes a semantic
communication-based edge–cloud framework for real-time urban
traffic digital twin applications. By leveraging edge intelligence
to extract and transmit only task-relevant semantic traffic information instead of full video feeds, the proposed framework
significantly reduces communication overhead while preserving
the essential dynamics needed for accurate real-time digital twin
reconstruction. Experimental results demonstrate more than 99%
transmission savings and an average trajectory reconstruction
error of only 0.12 m, confirming the proposed framework as an
effective, scalable, and low-latency solution for AI-enabled urban
traffic digital twin applications.
Index Terms—Semantic Communication, Digital-Twin, Urban
Traffic, Homograph, High Fidelity, Edge-Cloud communication.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Rapid urbanization and increasing traffic density have made
real-time traffic monitoring, collision prevention, and congestion mitigation essential in Intelligent Transportation Systems
(ITS). Although AI has shown strong potential for urban traffic
management, its performance depends on diverse and highquality datasets that capture critical events such as collisions,
near-collisions, off-lane behavior, and traffic rule violations.
In practice, collecting such comprehensive datasets from realworld surveillance systems remains difficult, especially for rare
yet safety-critical events.
Digital twin technology offers a promising solution by
creating a synchronized virtual replica of a physical traffic
environment. By reproducing roads, vehicles, traffic lights,
pedestrians, and other dynamic elements in simulation, digital
twins enable controlled scenario generation, large-scale data

strategies [1]. Thus, they provide an effective link between realworld traffic systems and AI-driven decision-making. However,
existing urban traffic digital twin studies often overlook the
combined challenges of real-time synchronization, realistic environmental modeling, and communication (comm.) efficiency.
In particular, continuously transmitting raw video streams from
edge surveillance cameras to the cloud creates substantial
bandwidth (BW) and latency overhead, limiting scalability and
real-time operation. In addition, insufficient physical realism in
vehicle behavior, actor placement, and trajectory consistency
can reduce the reliability of the virtual representation.
To address these issues, this paper proposes a semantic
comm.-enhanced edge-cloud digital twin framework for realtime urban traffic management. Building on our prior work on
urban environment representation [2], the proposed framework
reconstructs real traffic scenes on a cloud-hosted digital twin
while transmitting only task-relevant semantic information extracted at the edge, rather than full camera feeds. Lightweight
object detection modules integrated with surveillance cameras
capture the essential properties of real-world actors, significantly reducing comm. overhead while preserving the dynamics
needed for accurate reconstruction.
The proposed methodology consists of three stages. First, a
lightweight object detection model deployed on edge surveillance cameras extracts bounding box coordinates of real-world
traffic actors, such as vehicles. Second, these coordinates,
together with vehicle tracking information, are semantically
transmitted over the wireless edge-cloud link. Third, the cloud
server reconstructs the real traffic scene in the digital twin by
mapping image coordinates into simulation coordinates through
a planar homography matrix derived from normal driving
trajectory data [3]. In addition, the virtual platform incorporates
advanced physical modeling capabilities to represent vehicle
motion, collision behavior, road interactions, and environmental
responses more realistically. The main contributions of this
paper are summarized as follows:
We propose a semantic comm.-enhanced digital twin
framework for constructing a realistic urban traffic environment on a virtual platform, enabling intelligent traffic
management and safety-oriented urban mobility research.
• We integrate semantic comm. into the edge-cloud pipeline
to transmit only task-relevant traffic information instead of
raw video, achieving more than 99% transmission savings
and reducing comm. overhead and latency.
•

We employ a planar homography-based mapping mechanism to transform real-world pixel coordinates into simulation coordinates, enabling accurate reconstruction of
vehicle positions and activities in the digital twin with
an average trajectory error of 0.12 m.

II. R ELATED W ORKS
Digital Twin methodologies have been widely studied
throughout the literature due to their capacity to create virtual representations of real-world systems, thereby enabling
efficient monitoring, simulation, analysis, and optimization.
A researcher in [4] proposed the FlowTwin framework to
address the challenges of validation costs and scalability
issues in Digital twin development by introducing a dualphase approach to MoDT development. Another study in [5]
introduces a systematic, scalable methodology that employs
a modular software architecture, incorporating an AI-driven
sequential learning model to adapt dynamically to changing
traffic conditions. To minimize congestion and address the
complexities of large urban areas, researchers in [6] leverage
digital twin technologies called DT-STOP that use real-time
data from autonomous vehicles and roadside units. Moreover,
in [7], edge computing and deep learning are jointly employed
in digital twins to improve the real-time processing of traffic
information and the performance of traffic sign recognition.
Researchers in [8] focus on optimizing urban mobility holistically, considering urban subsystems that can be influenced by
factors such as weather, road hazards, traffic incidents, vehicle
emissions, and so on. Therefore, they proposed a case study for
Chattanooga in Tennessee with the Chattanooga Digital Twin,
CTwin, which enables situational awareness and cyber-physical
control. However, these studies mainly overlook comm. latency
and the minimum transmission payload during runtime, which
limits the digital twin’s adaptability to a real-time environment.
Semantic comm. is also studied across in digital twin domain. In [9], a semantic-driven traffic-generation framework
for digital twin networks is proposed that combines semantic
comm., Mininet-based simulation, and a transformer-based
diffusion model to generate high-fidelity network traffic at
lower comm. and computational costs. On the other hand,
researchers in [10] leverage a conditional generative semantic
comm. framework methodology in a digital twin network to
achieve reliable semantic recovery. Moreover, a point-cloudbased semantic comm. scheme for digital twins is introduced
in [11] to support efficient wireless transmission and accurate
3D scene generation under challenging channel conditions.
Minimizing the synchronization latency of all user devices
is studied in [12] by introducing a soft actor-critic-based
deep reinforcement learning algorithm in a semantic comm.based digital twin. Furthermore, a comprehensive framework

for cognitive semantic comm. is developed by research [13],
which optimizes key indicators such as semantic distortion,
entropy, resource efficiency, latency, and security in digital twin
platforms. Though these researches focus on the comm. part,
they are not mainly considering the generation of the proper
digital twin replica of the urban traffic.
In this work, we extend our previous study [2] by introducing
a real-time digital replica of the real world that is synchronized with low latency and BW efficient semantic edge-cloud
comm. The proposed framework addresses the gap between
computationally intensive high-fidelity traffic simulation and
the severe BW limitations of edge-to-cloud networks, while
enabling accurate reconstruction of a realistic urban traffic
digital twin from real-world observations.
III. M ETHODOLOGY
The proposed system architecture, which can be seen in
Fig.1, is divided into three steps: (i) edge devices, responsible
for semantic extraction via computer vision models that distill
each frame into a state vector, (ii) Edge-to-Cloud comm.
to modulate and transmit the semantic data, which is the
spatial and temporal information of the actors (eg. vehicles),
and (iii) a cloud server, responsible for spatial decoding and
physics-based reconstruction within BeamNG.tech simulation
platform [14]. The proposed workflow offloads environment reconstruction to the cloud while using semantic comm. to reduce
BW use and latency compared to raw video transmission.

B. Edge-Based Semantic Encoding of Traffic Images
The proposed system converts pixel-level traffic images into
a compact semantic payload by representing vehicles with
bounding boxes instead of raw image data. After capture
by edge cameras, the images are processed by a pre-trained
YOLOv11 model deployed on edge hardware, such as surveillance camera. For each frame t, the edge node transmits
only the encoded state vector St of each detected vehicle,
using bounding box coordinates, (ubox , vbox ), thereby reducing
redundant data and transmission overhead:
St = [t, ID, umin , vmin , umax , vmax , ucenter , vcenter , Class]

C. Spatial Decoding on the Cloud Server
Upon receiving the encoded state vector St , the cloud
server must decode them back into spatial world coordinates
(Xw , Yw , Zw ). Because the framework assumes no vehicles are
airborne, the predictive pipeline is strictly limited to Latitude
and Longitude equivalents, with elevation derived deterministically from the simulation environment itself.
1) 2D Planar Homography: Because vehicles are strictly
ground-bound, the mapping between the camera’s image plane
and the road surface can be described by a projective transformation between two planes. This relationship is captured by a
3 × 3 planar homography matrix H ∈ R3×3 , which encodes

the full perspective correspondence between pixel coordinates
and world coordinates on the ground plane.
A homography relates every point on one plane to a
unique point on another through a linear mapping in homogeneous coordinates. Given a pixel location (u, v) on the
image plane, its homogeneous representation is the threevector p̃ = [u, v, 1]⊤ . Applying H yields a transformed
homogeneous vector:

The nine entries of H encode three geometric operations
simultaneously: the upper-left 2 × 2 sub-matrix (h11 -h22 ) captures rotation and anisotropic scaling between the two planes;
the elements h13 and h23 represent translation; and the bottom
row (h31 , h32 , h33 ) introduces the projective component that
accounts for foreshortening, i.e., the effect by which objects
farther from the camera appear smaller in the image. Because
H is defined up to an arbitrary scale factor, it has eight degrees
of freedom and therefore requires a minimum of four noncollinear point correspondences to determine uniquely.
The output vector [x′ , y ′ , w′ ]⊤ is expressed in homogeneous coordinates, where the third component w′ is a projective
scaling factor that arises from the perspective division. It does
not correspond to a physical dimension; rather, it encodes the
depth-dependent scaling inherent in perspective projection. The
Euclidean world coordinates are recovered through dehomogenization:
X̂w = x′ /w′ ,

Ŷw = y ′ /w′

(10)

This division is the step that compensates for perspective distortion precisely because w′ varies across the image according
to the depth encoded by H. To estimate H, the Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC) algorithm is employed. RANSAC
operates iteratively: in each iteration, four point correspondences are sampled at random from the traversable environment
as pixel-world coordinate pairs, a candidate homography is
computed from these four points via Direct Linear Transform
(DLT), and the remaining correspondences are tested against
this candidate. Points whose re-projection error falls below a
predefined threshold of τ = 5.0 pixels are classified as inliers.

After a sufficient number of iterations, the candidate with
the largest inlier consensus set is selected. At inference time,
transforming a single pixel coordinate requires only one 3 × 3
matrix-vector multiplication and one scalar division, yielding
a computational complexity of O(1) per vehicle per frame.
2) Elevation Mapping: While planar homography provides
accurate 2D coordinate placements, real-world road topography
features variations in elevation, such as hills and cambers,
Operating under the strict kinematic assumption that passenger
vehicles remain persistently grounded, spatial elevation (Zw ) is
extracted deterministically using real-world Digital Elevation
Model (DEM) data or the target environment’s native terrain
heightmap. The elevation is retrieved via a direct spatial lookup
function E, which maps the decoded 2D coordinates to their
corresponding topographical height:
Ẑw = E(X̂w , Ŷw )

E. Edge-Cloud Communication
To evaluate the performance of Edge-to-Cloud communication, we model a 5G New Radio (NR) Frequency Range 1
(FR1) uplink channel. The system implements Low-Density
Parity-Check (LDPC) coding and a Hybrid Automatic Repeat
reQuest (HARQ) mechanism with Chase Combining [16].

1) Physical Layer and Error Modeling: The peak PHY layer
data rate RPHY is a function of the allocated channel BW (B =
100 MHz), the number of bits per symbol for the chosen modulation scheme (M ∈ {BPSK, QPSK, 16-QAM, 64-QAM}),
the LDPC coding rate (Rc = 0.5), and a fractional overhead η
accounting for guard bands and pilot signals.
We model the wireless link as an Additive White Gaussian
Noise (AWGN) channel. To accurately reflect 5G NR channel
performance, an empirical LDPC coding gain is applied to the
base Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). The theoretical uncoded Bit
Error Rate (BER) is determined by the effective SNR and the
specific modulation constellation. Data is segmented into fixedsize Transport Blocks (TB), and the Block Error Rate (BLER)
is derived assuming independent bit errors within a block.
2) HARQ with Chase Combining: To ensure reliable payload delivery over the fading channel, our model implements a
HARQ protocol utilizing Chase Combining (CC). In HARQCC, the receiver buffers the soft information of corrupted
transport blocks and combines them with subsequent retransmissions. We model this energy accumulation as a linear
effective SNR gain in the logarithmic domain (GCC in dB) for
each retransmission round k ∈ {0, 1, . . . , Kmax }. Consequently,
the probability of a block failing decreases with each successive
HARQ round.
3) System-Level Latency and Goodput: Difference between
the transmission of raw images and YOLO CSV data yields
vastly different payload sizes (D), defining system-level metrics accurately is critical. A payload is segmented into Nblk
transport blocks. During the k-th HARQ round, only the subset

degradation caused by large payload failures.
F. End-to-End Computational Complexity Analysis
The proposed architecture distributes the computational load
asymmetrically. On the edge node, YOLOv11 inference is computed. Optimized for TensorRT or specific Neural Processing
Units (NPUs), this operation scales with the input resolution
O(W ×H) but requires extremely low memory BW. It entirely
avoids the high energy costs and thermal throttling associated
with deep hardware video encoding.

On the receiver side, the cloud node executes O(1) matrix
multiplications for the RANSAC homography decoding per
vehicle, performs an O(1) spatial memory lookup for deterministic Z-elevation mapping via the DEM, and hosts the
O(N ) BeamNG.tech soft-body physics engine for N vehicles. Consequently, the heavy, unbounded computational lifting
is safely offloaded to centralized cloud environments where
GPU/CPU resources, memory BW, and power consumption
are unconstrained, leaving only lightweight extraction tasks at
edge.

V. C ONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we proposed a semantic comm.-enhanced
digital twin framework for real-time urban traffic management.
By transmitting only task-relevant semantic information from
edge surveillance cameras to the cloud, the proposed system significantly reduced edge-cloud communication overhead
while preserving accurate and reliable synchronization between
the real-world environment and its digital replica. Experimental
results in BeamNG.tech physics-based simulation platform
validated the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The
planar homography-based reconstruction achieved a mean error
of only 0.1260 m, demonstrating accurate mapping of realworld observations into the simulation environment. In addition, the semantic comm. strategy achieved 99.99% memory
savings and reduced transmission latency from nearly 500 ms
to approximately 1 ms under favorable SNR conditions. The
framework also remained resilient in low-SNR settings, providing reliable synchronization even when conventional video
streaming becomes ineffective. Overall, these results show that
the proposed framework is an effective, scalable, and lowlatency solution for urban traffic digital twin applications. In
future work, we will use this framework to generate diverse
traffic scenarios, including collisions, traffic rule violations,
and other safety-critical events, to support the development
of advanced AI models for traffic flow optimization, collision
avoidance, and related intelligent transportation tasks.

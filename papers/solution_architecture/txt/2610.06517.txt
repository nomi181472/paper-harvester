Abstract
IoT encompasses a wide range of physical entities, from smart home devices to autonomous vehicles,
creating a complex environment that employs discrete security models to tackle its diversity. Due to the
heterogeneous nature of the IoT infrastructure, the sub-systems are often vulnerable to various network
attacks. To address these ongoing limitations, modern security systems must be more credible to ensure
security and privacy for IoT applications. Moreover, a highly secure IoT system demands real-time
insight, which calls for insight collection from decentralization of the edge of the computing layer. At
the same time, this diversity of the IoT ecosystem entails a unified security model that is applied at the
foundational level. One of the counterparts to this requirement is edge intelligence, which is considered
the direct approach to the diversity of the involved devices. One of the practical goals of edge intelligence
in the IoT ecosystem is to extract insight from local data; then the security model leverages the data
gathered from this stage to build local node protections, and its integration with AI models can present
an advanced security solution to this challenge. In this direction, the current research proposes a novel
deep learning algorithm utile for effective intrusions to detections on the edge of intelligence, supported by
a cloud-based IoT framework. This study evaluates the proposed cross-modal deep learning algorithm
and compares its performance against baseline models. Accordingly, the contribution of the current
research is a cross-domain Deep Neural Network (DNN) algorithm that facilitates intrusion detection.The
objective is to assess a multi-method deep learning model to detect intrusion in IoT systems on the edge of
intelligence based on detection of communities with modelled attention. In this context, the study evaluates
GCTauto-encoder, a novel framework that integrates edge intelligence within a comprehensive system to
identify security flaws. The proposed model demonstrates that it significantly improves performance and
efficiency. Evaluated on a network intrusion IoT dataset covering different network intrusion scenarios, it
achieved a 0.908 accuracy improvement over benchmark models, reduced learning loss to 0.00156, and
outperformed existing approaches within the evaluated setting.

Introduction

Internet of Things (IoT), expanding due to its capacity for integration with artificial Intelligence, has expanded
the application of IoT to machinery data, data-driven techniques, modern networks, mutual services across
industries, and is promoting further expansion [1]. IoT has notable applications across smart healthcare, smart
home, smart transportation, and smart industry—with fine-grained practices seen in areas like localization,
predictive monitoring, autonomous driving, and intelligent control. A robust IoT ecosystem must establish
credibility through strong security and privacy measures. Given the diverse network architecture of IoT with

numerous nodes, these systems are vulnerable to various cyber-attacks, especially due to the massive data
flows they generate over time. To protect IoT applications, traditional security measures must be strengthened
to address system-specific vulnerabilities of IoT [2]. The IoT’s vast network has a dynamic nature where
intrusion risks necessitate robust security frameworks, especially in sensitive areas like healthcare, where
data privacy is crucial. In this regard, edge computing and artificial intelligence in the paradigm of edge of

Literature Review

The Internet of Things is no longer in perspective, and it has a key constituent in the billions of communication
units. The far-reaching targets of closing the gap between physical and virtual entities are in close proximity
in the current area. This opens numerous gates for integrations of artificial intelligence, while its production
of massive data circulations imposes a demand for real-time data analysis. As IoT ecosystems have grown
in complexity, security models have shifted accordingly. Earlier approaches relied on unified, one-size-fitsall mechanisms applied at the foundational level. However, as the diversity of devices, from healthcare
systems to autonomous vehicles, expanded, the limitations of such uniform models became apparent. A
growing body of research work has moved toward discrete security models that operate locally, extracting
insight from device-level data and progressively analyzing them as a whole [3]. This view is supported by
studies demonstrating that direct, localized approaches are better suited to heterogeneous network security
architectures [4]. Proceeding literature has concentrated on methods to secure IoT wireless network paradigms
and expand its high capacity even further, leveraging Artificial intelligence. Based on this, integration
of machine learning models can be used as catalysts for fast detection of security flaws. This provides
a systematic understanding of better approaches revolves around three broad themes that emerge from
this literature on modern AI security models [5]: (i) device-level defence with system-wide synthesis, (ii)
integration of adaptive AI techniques, and (iii) the privacy–functionality trade-off in large-scale networks.
Moving forward, recent research suggests the establishment of clusters of controllers, each responsible for
specific decision-making tasks—a concept that maps naturally onto AI, where each controller functions as
an intelligent agent processing sensor data from its sub-ecosystem. To maintain real-time security across
the system, a dynamic slave controller can be assigned within each cluster of nodes. This continuously
assesses task allocation, circulation, and migration as network conditions change [6]. An instance of such
a network modelling is HetNets [7], whose functionality relies on seamless coordination between different
tiers of base stations, while their security is challenged by the expanded attack surface introduced by densely
distributed access points [8]. Such a network presents multiple security challenges, including the need for
accurate identification of threats across the system [9], [10]. These concerns, though exemplified in specific

2

network architectures, are part of a wider set of challenges driving the field. Furthermore, the trajectory of
research in this area reflects a move toward an intelligent model that is capable of more precise detection
and classification of security events across the system [11]. On the edge of intelligence, one of the most
important procedures is the selection of an optimum frameworks or models to further harvest the security
mechanism and to implement the most suitable artificial intelligence solutions to investigate the underlying
patterns in the sensors’ collected data. In this domain, the earliest AI models applied to network security
were rule-based expert systems [12], which encoded human knowledge to detect known attack patterns [13,
14]. As IoT ecosystems grew in complexity, however, a new approach became necessary: a large-scale entity
that cooperates through localization-based methods to achieve system completion. These were followed by
shallow machine learning classifiers, such as decision trees, support vector machines, and naive Bayes trained
on labelled traffic data for intrusion detection. Neural networks with a single hidden layer later enabled more
flexible pattern recognition, including anomaly detection [15] beyond predefined signatures. Despite their
limitations, these early models laid the foundation for today’s deep learning approaches by demonstrating
that automated, data-driven methods could identify malicious activity without explicit programming. A later
transfer-based approach for the same fault was employed in [16]. In earlier attack detection and fault-diagnosis
pipelines, the Wavelet transform constitutes the primary preprocessing step [17], extracting time–frequency
representations from raw vibration signals. These representations are subsequently classified using soft-max
regression, which provides the baseline linear model in the overall framework. Finally, variational and
stacked auto-encoders (SAE) enable unsupervised feature learning through stacked reconstruction, while
DNN advances further into fully end-to-end supervised deep learning, together capturing the progression from
shallow signal processing to modern deep learning for bearing fault diagnosis. In a later progressive solution,
a transfer learning-based approach was investigated. Additionally, in cases where a larger amount of data
processing was required, Bayesian [18], Weibull distribution, and deep belief network (DBN) were applied
to evaluate the bearing degradations [19], [20]. In short, the literature on IoT ecosystems has evolved from
unified security models toward localized, device-specific approaches, driven by the growing diversity of devices
and data flows. However, existing studies have typically implemented security controls and data-analytics
pipelines in isolation, resulting in systems where intrusion detection, encryption, or access control are designed
without regard to how diagnostic or predictive models consume the same operational data [7]. This gap
motivates the present study, which investigates how graph modelling of deep learning approaches equipped
with contrastive learning can bridge security and local and global data analysis in heterogeneous IoT network
environments and their protection systems.

Problem Definition

While the above approaches and technologies enable high capability of IoT systems in modern networks,
they still face a demand for effective decision-making in relation to IoT security vulnerabilities. A key
limitation of conventional IoT networks is that each node operates based on only local information [26].
While individual nodes may possess valuable insights about their immediate environment, there has not been
systematic research work to consolidate these fragmented perspectives [27] [28] into a coherent global view.
Accordingly, the following problem arises when the system does not benefit from both local responsiveness and
centralized aggregation. The problem becomes more pronounced when the typical limitations of a centralized
computing architecture lack contextual knowledge [29], delays in centralized reporting, alarm parallelization,
and inadequate real-time notifications. These issues are considered security flaws in this structure. If the focus
is on a centralized computing architecture for IoT, it enforces restrictions on IoT platforms, particularly when
different distributed components are involved. The aim is to pass the detection to a system that first assess
on local and then leverage the knowledge as a whole. A centrally placed model that receives inputs from all
local nodes can effectively act as the aggregation point. In this manner, the approach benefits from both
centralized and decentralized approaches. Conventional security systems have limited credibility to provide a
solid foundation for security and privacy in IoT applications. On the other hand, the Intrusion Detection
Systems (IDS) and Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS) [30] must be designed in a way that is intelligent
and distributed within modern cloud-based IoT platforms. This means that the components of the IDS and
IPS can vary across different sections of the platform. The goal is to transition to a centralized architecture
for the controller to streamline operations. This becomes increasingly critical as variation in IoT scenarios
and requirements grows. In the IoT ecosystem, Distributed Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) equipped
with Edge Intelligence (EI) [31] are essential for protection against Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)
attacks and their variations. Moreover, making IDS and Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS) autonomous
in their decision-making regarding local protection is a key approach to creating highly secure IoT systems.
As mentioned, a secure IoT system demands real-time insights, which necessitates the decentralization of
the edge computing layer. For instance, consider an autonomous guided vehicle that continuously collects
sensor data regarding activity patterns, speed, and other metrics from various devices. This data is processed
to generate a replica model of the current situation. Data collection in these scenarios is often sensitive, so
the process of data acquisition must be secure. Another aspect is that different types of credentials, such as

5

A05 for application and service credentials and A06 for physical entity data credentials, must be handled
distinctly. Consequently, without a unified approach managing these categories becomes increasingly complex.
These internet security credentials help protect against network intrusion attacks, and integrated AI models
must perform based on these protocol credentials. In response to these challenges, the proposed model in
this regard performs based on local anomaly scoring, where each node computes a lightweight anomaly score
(e.g., deviation from local traffic baseline) and flags suspicious activity; local feature extraction. Each node
extracts statistical features such as packet rate, protocol distribution, and entropy and sends them upstream.
Local threshold filtering meaning, each node applies node-level threshold conditions (e.g., packet count against
threshold, unknown destination) and only forwards flagged events to the central system. Local model inference
on node-level produces a preliminary classification known as benign versus suspicious, then the central system
aggregates these decisions. One of the main goals of such modelling is learning the network communication
patterns at the edges of IoT locations. This allows for the identification of the corresponding network topology
known as communities. Since it is key to recognize that the communication topology typically revolves around
continuous-time data, which includes multiple variables. This factor is crucial for effective preventive measures
against IoT network intrusion attacks. In a nutshell, the proposed model operates on the following principle:
On Local level it reduces bandwidth, enables real-time response, preserves privacy and on the Central level:
sees the full picture, detects distributed attacks across various protocols, handles complex patterns. To extend
this solution, the proposed approach incorporates additional mechanisms that strengthen its effectiveness.

Methodology

To address these limitations in overall system security, the current study introduces an artificial model with
the capacity to operate seamlessly at the edge of intelligence, providing actionable insights for SCADA systems.
This is done by leveraging multi-attention-head graph-based foundational model that learns using data acquired
through wireless sensors. The proposed approach can improve the responsiveness and operational efficiency
of the ecosystem. To overcome mentioned constraints, the objective is to detect multiple security flaws and
provide insights in the edge intelligence for consolidated decision-making as a whole. On this basis, within the
broader family of neural network models, supervised approaches such as CNNs and RNNs require large labelled
datasets. These datasets are costly to obtain and process based on the network traffic patterns for training such
a model. Instead, self-supervised models such as auto-encoders detect anomalies by learning the distribution
of normal behaviour and their deviations in reconstruction indicate attacks and drive knowledge from the
labelled data patterns. However, standard auto-encoders treat nodes independently, ignoring communication
topology. To address these challenges, current work proposes a variational contrastive auto-encoder modelled
with a multi-attention-head graph architecture [32], [33]. The attention graph modelling has the capacity
to detect the communities. The formulation detects communities since the attention weights are driven by

the element-wise interaction A ⊙ σ XW X H T W H , which amplifies node pairs that are both structurally
connected and feature-compatible. As a result, nodes inside the same community receive consistently higher
aggregated signals, causing the heads to converge toward coherent sub-graph-level patterns. The proposed
model, GCT auto-encoder, overcomes this by weighting neighbour contributions via attention, capturing
relational structure in the graph. Here A is the adjacency matrix, X is the node feature matrix, H represents
community assignment embeddings, ⊙ denotes element-wise multiplication, and σ is an activation function.
The attention scores are masked by the adjacency structure so that nodes only attend to their direct neighbours,
and the output H encodes soft community membership probabilities across h attention heads. Therefore, it is

Contrastive learning works by pulling similar items closer together in the representation space and pushing
dissimilar items apart. The contrastive loss in (3) does this by computing, for each “anchor” sample. The
loss is computed as the negative log of the ratio between the anchor’s similarity to its positive pair and
the sum of its similarities to all negative samples. When the anchor is substantially more similar to the
positive than to any negative, this ratio becomes large, and the resulting loss is low; conversely, a small
ratio yields a higher loss. Equation (4) computes the instance-level contrastive loss ιi,j between a specific
sample pair (i, j). Here sim(·, ·) is a similarity function—typically cosine similarity, which measures the angle
between two vectors and ranges from −1 to 1. The temperature parameter τ > 0 controls how “peaked” the
similarity distribution is: a low temperature amplifies differences between similarities, making the loss focus
more aggressively on the hardest negatives, while a high temperature spreads things out more evenly and
treats negatives more uniformly. The indicator function 1{k̸=i} ensures the anchor itself is excluded from the
denominator—otherwise the denominator would count a perfect match with itself, trivializing the loss. In

Dataset Properties and Preprocessing Considerations

The dataset used in this study is the IoT network traffic dataset by Kang et al. [39], which contains 42 raw
network packet capture (PCAP) files collected at different time points. The packets were captured using a
wireless network adapter in monitor mode, and the wireless headers were removed using Aircrack-ng, yielding
standard network-layer packets. The contributor of this dataset generated all attack categories—except the
Mirai Botnet—were by simulating attacks with tools such as Nmap. For the Mirai Botnet category, the
dataset creators generated the malicious traffic on a laptop and subsequently modified the packets to make
them appear as if they originated from an IoT device. The statistical properties in this IoT dataset include
features such as the number of packets sent, the average packet length, the count of unique destinations,

Sensitivity Overview

Sensitivity analysis was conducted to examine how variations in key hyperparameters and architectural choices
influence model behaviour. The evaluation focused on parameters that typically affect stability, including
learning rate, regularization strength, and the weighting of auxiliary losses. Each parameter was perturbed
within a controlled range while all other components remained fixed. The resulting trends show that moderate
deviations lead to predictable shifts in performance, whereas extreme settings cause degradation in both
accuracy and convergence reliability. These observations indicate that the model maintains robustness within
its recommended operating range and that the selected hyperparameters form a balanced configuration.

10

Error Analysis

Error patterns were analysed to identify systematic failure modes across ablated variants. Misclassification
were grouped by category, input characteristics, and proximity to decision boundaries. Ablated models showed
increased errors in borderline or ambiguous cases, reflecting reduced discriminative strength when certain
components were removed. Some variants also displayed heightened sensitivity to noise or infrequent patterns.
The full model produced fewer clustered errors and a more uniform distribution, indicating that its integrated
design mitigates several weaknesses observed in the ablations.

Conclusion and Discussion

The current research presented a novel approach to learning compressed representations from the IoT dataset
using an information bottleneck strategy. The proposed method, GCTAuto-encoder, effectively balances the
trade-off between compression and reconstruction fidelity. This indicates that the model is enable of compact
yet informative feature extraction from high-dimensional inputs. Experimental results demonstrate that the
proposed model achieves strong performance of 91.6 percent across various attacks, outperforming baseline
methods in F1, AUC-ROC, speed, and robustness. The learned representations successfully discard redundant
information while retaining task-relevant features, validating the effectiveness of the information bottleneck
principle. Future work could explore adaptive bottleneck capacity and extend the framework to multi-modal
settings. The results shows that this approach offers a promising direction for efficient representation learning
in high-dimensional domains. The proposed model GCTauto-encoder achieves the lowest train loss (0.00156)
and test loss (0.00251) among all models, with a very low over-fitting score and very high stability. This
signifies a high generalization capacity and consistent performance across data splits. Its generalization gap of
0.00095 is nearly the smallest in the table, second only to Model contractive variation (0.00091), which also
scores very low over-fitting and very high stability. The proposed model ranks first overall, achieving the
highest scores in the reported measures. With this measurement it surpasses all benchmark models across
every metric. In sum, the evidence reflects a method that maintains dependable behaviour across the tested
scenarios.

Summary of contributions

This work addresses the problem of security flaw detection in IoT systems by introducing a cross domain deep
learning framework that based on the core structure of bottle architecture of auto-encoder. The proposed
framework incorporates multi-attention graph modelling for detecting the communities and contrastive loss to

19

overcome the limitations supervised learning techniques. Experimental results demonstrate that the method
achieves high performance and robustness, projecting its effectiveness for adaptation in edge on intelligence
system in IoT networks.

11.2

Limitations and future work

The model’s robustness was examined under controlled perturbations, but its behaviour under real-world
distribution shifts remains insufficiently characterized. Future work could investigate robustness under domain
shift and evaluate distributed training strategies. Extending the framework to streaming or online-learning
scenarios also remains an open challenge.
